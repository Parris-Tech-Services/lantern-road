# Quest and World Callback Blocks

Use these as modular epilogue paragraphs. Select only the strongest relevant blocks.

# Lanterns on the Old Road

## Bargained

Caravans use the old road again.

People still tell the story differently.

In Hearthwick, the party is remembered as the group that broke the false-lantern crew's bluff without leaving bodies under the Weeping Stones.

Some call that restraint.

Crow calls it a calculated risk that happened to keep holding.

A few former lantern runners find legitimate work.

At least one does not.

The road is safer.

It is not innocent.

## Fought clear

The false lanterns disappear quickly after the fight at the Weeping Stones.

Merchants trust the route sooner when fear has a visible ending.

So do people looking for revenge.

Hearthwick benefits from the safer traffic.

Rowan never lets “necessary” become a synonym for “good.”

## Unresolved

Travellers still talk about lights near the stones.

Perhaps the crew moved on.

Perhaps the fear simply became part of the road.

Hearthwick learned to plan around uncertainty.

That is a kind of damage too.

# The Missing Ledger

## Returned to the Gilt Caravan Guild

Oswin Marris gets the ledger back.

Greyfen's accounts become quieter.

Not cleaner.

Quieter.

The Guild corrects several practices and buries several arguments in meetings the public never hears.

People who value stable trade call that responsible custody.

People who wanted accountability use less polite language.

## Given to the Ashen Veil

The ledger stops existing as one object.

Rumours suggest pages were copied.

Names disappear from some versions and become very prominent in others.

The Ashen Veil protects several people who might otherwise have been exposed.

It also gains leverage no public office can audit.

## Archived as evidence

Candlemere gives the ledger a number.

That sounds small.

It changes everything.

The Guild can argue context.

The Wardens can dispute interpretation.

Nera can complain about dangerous names being preserved.

Nobody can plausibly say the document never existed.

## Lost / unresolved

The ledger becomes one more story about a dangerous document nobody can produce.

People quote lines they may never have read.

Power survives comfortably inside that uncertainty.

# The Pilgrim's Reliquary

## Returned to Saint Rhel's Shrine

The reliquary goes home.

Pilgrims begin stopping again, some for faith and some because stories are a reason to rest tired feet.

Elira keeps the object more carefully than before.

“Home” does not become an excuse for negligence.

## Archived in Candlemere

The reliquary survives behind good glass.

Scholars can document it.

Pilgrims can request to see it.

Sister Elira remains genuinely glad it is safe and genuinely unhappy it is gone.

Both feelings last.

## Left hidden / unresolved

Saint Rhel's chamber remains sealed.

That protects the reliquary for now.

It also leaves the next decision to whoever finds it later.

# Fever on the South Road

## Delivered in time

Yor Dane never says the party saved Alderwatch.

He says the shelves did not go empty.

That is more precise.

People recover who might not have.

Other people remain sick.

The road did what a road is supposed to do:

it carried help before help became history.

## Late but useful

The crate arrives late enough that nobody calls it a victory.

It still gets opened.

It still gets used.

Alderwatch remembers both facts.

The party learns that an imperfect success does not become failure merely because regret is present.

## Diverted

The medicine reaches people.

Just not the people who were promised it.

In the marsh, several families remember unexpected help.

In Alderwatch, empty shelves become part of the story.

No ending should convert one group into proof that the other did not matter.

## Too late

The medicine is useful for tomorrow.

That is what Yor says.

Nobody uses the phrase “too late” to describe the people who are still there.

The people who were lost remain part of Alderwatch's memory of the road.

## Refused / unresolved

Someone else may have tried the run.

The ending should state what is actually known, not invent closure.

If Alderwatch's final state is uncertain:

> The south road keeps its unanswered question.

# The Silent Tower

## Full chart recovered

The Moonmere chart becomes difficult evidence.

Old ferries existed.

Seasonal roads were once ordinary.

Current bottlenecks were built over time, not handed down by geography.

The chart does not tell the March what to do.

It makes several people stop saying there was never another choice.

## Partial / damaged chart

Enough survives to weaken certainty.

Not enough survives to settle every claim.

Sen Marrow considers this scientifically normal and emotionally infuriating.

## Field copies distributed

Fragments of Moonmere's old routes appear in ferrymen's booths, Warden reports and travellers' notebooks before the Archive finishes arguing about them.

Some prove useful.

One sends three people to a bank Vesk immediately calls “historically accurate and currently terrible.”

## Unrecovered

Moonmere Tower still leans above the reeds.

People continue arguing about what its chart might have shown.

Absence becomes part of the politics.

# Ash in the Marsh

## Exposed to the Wardens

Warden patrols make several Mosslight routes harder to use.

One predatory route disappears entirely.

Two useful routes become more dangerous for people who depended on staying unnoticed.

Edda calls the result actionable.

Nera calls it predictable.

## Brokered with the Ashen Veil

The truce around Mosslight is never officially signed because officially it does not exist.

Medicine crosses.

Some seizures stop.

Some contraband keeps moving too.

The arrangement survives because everyone involved finds at least one part of it irritating.

## Recorded as evidence

Mosslight stops being a ghost story.

Once the routes, sigils and movement patterns are documented, every faction has to admit an organised network exists.

They still disagree about what that fact requires.

## Violence / partial resolution

After violence at Mosslight, the lights vanish from the obvious places.

They do not vanish from the marsh.

The network becomes harder to read, more suspicious and less centralised.

Breaking a meeting is not the same thing as solving the need that created it.

## Unresolved

Green lights still move in the reeds.

Some nights they carry medicine.

Some nights nobody knows.
