# Party Epilogue Blocks

These are modular character endings for LR-0008.

They should be selected by Trust, relationship state and LR-0044 personal-arc outcome when available.

Do not repeat full personal quest scenes.

# Garrick Vale

## High Trust, personal arc unresolved

Garrick still wakes before everyone else.

He still checks the road twice.

What changes is smaller.

Sometimes, when someone says they have the watch, he believes them for several minutes at a time.

The March has not cured him of responsibility.

It has begun teaching him that responsibility can have more than one pair of hands.

## Low Trust

Garrick remains dependable.

That is not the same as close.

When the campaign ends, he packs the party's gear carefully and asks what road comes next.

He does not ask whether everyone is staying together.

### Personal outcome — Shared Watch

At the next crowded crossing, Garrick does not step immediately into the worst position.

He looks around.

Assigns three jobs.

Keeps one for himself.

Perrin Dace would recognise the difference.

### Personal outcome — Held Line

Garrick still believes there are nights when one person simply has to stand.

He also knows, now, that being right about the night does not answer the morning.

### Personal outcome — Closed Crossing

Garrick passes one road he once would have tried to save.

He looks back.

Then keeps walking.

> “People got home.”

It is not certainty.

It is enough to take the next step.

# Mira Quickstep

## High Trust, personal arc unresolved

Mira remains the first person to notice an exit.

She becomes slightly less likely to use it simply because it exists.

When somebody asks where she is from, she still gives different answers.

The party learns which ones are jokes.

## Low Trust

Mira stays useful, funny and difficult to pin down.

When the campaign ends, she knows everyone well enough to leave without a scene.

Whether she does is a different question.

### Personal outcome — Buried Name

Nobody publicly connects Mira Quickstep to Mira Venn.

That is the outcome she chose.

It protects her.

It also leaves a blank space where other people will sometimes write their own story.

Lio sends one message addressed simply:

> Mira.

She keeps it.

### Personal outcome — Named Truth

The name **Mira Venn** appears in more places than Mira enjoys.

The sky does not fall.

Some old problems become easier to find.

So do some old friends.

When someone uses the name unexpectedly, she still flinches.

Then answers.

### Personal outcome — Shared Boundary

Lio keeps Venn.

Mira keeps Quickstep.

Between them sits one written line:

> No inherited accounts.

It does not solve history.

It tells history where the boundary is.

# Oren Thale

## High Trust, personal arc unresolved

Oren keeps notes on the campaign.

He stops pretending they are a neutral record.

There are more phrases like:

> Mira disagreed.

> Garrick warned us.

> Brindle was right about the cost.

He still loves precision.

He has learned that precision includes recording who was allowed to speak.

## Low Trust

Oren remains courteous and interested.

His notebook contains fewer personal observations.

At the end of the March, he thanks the party in language so formal that Mira later asks whether he accidentally resigned from them.

### Personal outcome — Retracted

Oren's retraction remains in the Archive.

He dislikes how cleanly it reads.

That discomfort becomes useful.

When a future report says a harmful decision was “reasonable under available evidence,” he asks:

> Available to whom?

### Personal outcome — Amended

Mara Kett's warning sits beside Oren's original recommendation.

Neither erases the other.

Oren becomes irritatingly enthusiastic about margins.

### Personal outcome — Mara's Account

The scorched page is no longer in Oren's coat.

For weeks, his hand still checks the empty pocket.

Then one day it does not.

Mara's account exists under Mara's name.

The Archive is not the only place truth survived.

# Sister Brindle

## High Trust, personal arc unresolved

Brindle still prays for people by name.

She becomes more willing to include herself.

Not every night.

Enough to notice.

When the campaign's final choice leaves someone dissatisfied, she does not rush to explain why it was necessary.

She listens first.

## Low Trust

Brindle remains kind.

Kindness is not intimacy.

At the end of the March she blesses the party's next road, including the possibility that it may not be the same road.

### Personal outcome — Child

Brindle never turns the saved child into proof that the decision was right.

When someone praises the choice, she says:

> “It was a compassionate choice.”

Then, if needed:

> “That is different.”

### Personal outcome — Scout

Brindle accepts that helping the person who keeps a route open can be mercy.

She also keeps asking who gets described as “strategic” and who simply gets described as waiting.

### Personal outcome — Pilgrims

Brindle remembers the stranded pilgrims whenever somebody reduces the March to freight, evidence or patrol coverage.

> “Somebody has to remember the people who are bad arguments.”

The line becomes less bitter each time she uses it.

# Relationship pair callbacks

Use at most one strong pair callback per ending unless the relationship system becomes much richer.

## Garrick + Mira — strong trust

At the next fork, Garrick asks Mira which route she would take before giving his own answer.

Mira notices.

She makes no joke about it until the following morning.

## Garrick + Mira — friction

They still disagree about whether a good plan should be direct or difficult to predict.

The difference now is that neither mistakes the disagreement for surprise.

## Oren + Brindle — strong trust

Oren asks Brindle to read a final account before he files it.

She crosses out one sentence.

He argues for ten minutes.

Then leaves it crossed out.

## Oren + Brindle — friction

Brindle thinks Oren still hides moral choices inside careful words.

Oren thinks Brindle sometimes asks uncertainty to do work evidence should do.

Both accusations occasionally land.

That is why the argument survives.
