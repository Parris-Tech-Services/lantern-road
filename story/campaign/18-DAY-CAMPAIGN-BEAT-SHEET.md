# Lantern Road — 18-Day Campaign Beat Sheet

**Task:** LR-0043 — Author the 18-day campaign beat sheet and ending matrix  
**Owner:** Agent 2 — The Storyteller  
**Status:** Authored blueprint only. Runtime/save integration belongs to LR-0008 after the foundation gates.

## Campaign promise

Lantern Road should feel like a short tabletop campaign in which the player gradually learns one small region well enough to understand the human cost of its roads.

The campaign question is:

> **When every route is scarce, who gets to decide what the road is for?**

The answer should emerge from the six existing questlines rather than from a separate “main quest.” The road threat, missing ledger, reliquary, medicine run, Moonmere chart and Mosslight dispute all become different views of the same frontier problem: **movement is power**.

A road carries more than travellers. It determines who receives medicine, which market survives, whose records become law, which shrine remains reachable, whose patrols arrive first and which people can still move when official routes fail.

The campaign should never reveal a single secret mastermind. The stronger truth is less tidy: the Grey March is brittle because several reasonable institutions are trying to solve scarcity in incompatible ways.

## Narrative thesis

A useful internal line for the whole campaign:

> **A road is a promise between people who may not like each other.**

Each faction protects a real good and creates a real danger:

- **Gilt Caravan Guild:** reliable trade and logistics; danger of making access answer to profit and leverage.
- **Wardens of the Green March:** safety and emergency response; danger of turning temporary control into permanent authority.
- **Archive of Candlemere:** memory, evidence and durable rules; danger of letting records outrank the people living beside them.
- **Ashen Veil:** access where official systems fail; danger of making essential routes depend on secrecy, favours and unaccountable brokers.

None should be written as the “evil faction.” A premium-feeling ending comes from choosing what cost the player is willing to accept.

## Structural rules

The 18-day spine should create escalation without turning the sandbox into a calendar checklist.

1. **Days are pressure windows, not mandatory quest orders.** The player may approach settlements and quests in different sequences.
2. **Major beats use soft triggers.** If the player misses the ideal location, a rumour, messenger, camp conversation or settlement callback can deliver the necessary information later.
3. **Existing quests remain independently meaningful.** They feed the larger campaign but should still work as satisfying local stories.
4. **Renown is legitimacy, not a morality score.** High renown means people will hear the party out when the region needs a decision. It does not mean the party made “good” choices.
5. **Named choices must echo.** At least one later line, service state, route condition, relationship response or ending callback should remember each major quest outcome.
6. **The late choice is about governance under scarcity, not defeating a final boss.**
7. **The final two days should feel urgent without invalidating exploration.** The player can still travel, but the March should sound and look like a region bracing for fewer usable routes.

# Act I — Learn the Road
## Days 1–4

### Purpose

Introduce the Grey March at human scale. The player should initially believe they are solving unrelated local problems.

The opening emotional rhythm is:

**small request → road problem → human consequence → first remembered choice**

### Day 1 — Hearthwick: somebody still has to leave first

**Preferred starting material**

- Hearthwick
- Rowan Pike
- Tessa Reed
- Sister Elira
- **Lanterns on the Old Road**
- **The Pilgrim's Reliquary**

The opening should stress that Hearthwick is safe only because other people keep reaching it.

Suggested opening texture:

- carts waiting longer than usual;
- Tessa stretching stores because fewer travellers are arriving;
- Rowan worrying about the false lanterns without having enough people to patrol every road;
- Elira treating the loss of a reliquary as partly a question of whether small places still matter when resources are scarce.

**Player-facing idea:** the map may look open, but every choice of route costs daylight, food and attention.

### Day 2 — The road becomes specific

The player should encounter the first evidence that danger on the road is often made by people, not monsters.

Ideal material:

- Weeping Stones;
- Watcher's Rest;
- Saint Rhel's Shrine;
- first travel/camp conversation;
- first clue toward Greyfen or Broken Span.

The false-lantern crew should establish an important campaign pattern: someone can exploit a weak route without being the cause of the whole region's weakness.

### Day 3 — First visible consequence

If the player resolves an early quest, the world should acknowledge it quickly.

Examples:

- caravans cautiously return after the false-lantern threat changes;
- pilgrims begin using Saint Rhel's Shrine again if the reliquary returns;
- Brindle, Garrick, Mira or Oren react to the method used.

If the player has not resolved anything yet, show **cost of delay**, not punishment:

- an inn room mentions a caravan that turned back;
- a traveller chooses a longer path;
- a local says they cannot tell whether the road is dangerous or merely unreliable.

### Day 4 — Widen the map

Rumours should now pull toward at least two of:

- Greyfen Market;
- Broken Span;
- the missing ledger;
- the medicine run;
- Blacksalt Crossing.

The player should feel they are choosing **which problem matters first**, not choosing “main quest versus side quest.”

# Act II — Every Favour Belongs to Someone
## Days 5–8

### Purpose

Make the player understand that neutral help is becoming difficult. Solving one person's problem changes another person's leverage.

### Day 5 — Greyfen's arithmetic

Greyfen is the best place to make institutions visible.

Use:

- Oswin Marris;
- Halden Crow;
- Joric Pell;
- **The Missing Ledger**;
- **Fever on the South Road**.

Three different kinds of urgency should overlap:

- Oswin wants dangerous information controlled.
- Crow wants order and accountability.
- Joric wants a physical crate moved before delay becomes suffering.

The player should notice that all three can sincerely say, “This cannot wait.”

### Day 6 — Deadline with a face

The medicine run should make time pressure personal.

Do not frame the deadline only as a timer. Give it texture:

- Alderwatch is counting bandages;
- Yor Dane has already divided supplies by who is most likely to survive;
- a caravan master knows exactly how many hours a detour costs.

If the crate is diverted, delayed or delivered, that choice should later appear in the campaign's moral memory.

### Day 7 — The ledger stops being paperwork

The missing ledger should reveal that records can reroute power as effectively as a barricade.

Possible truths carried by the ledger:

- disputed toll payments;
- favoured inspections;
- payments that reveal which routes were deliberately neglected;
- names that could embarrass the Gilt Caravan Guild, Wardens of the Green March or private operators.

Important: the ledger must **complicate** faction claims, not prove one faction secretly caused everything.

### Day 8 — A quieter road before the turn

Give the campaign one breathing beat.

Good material:

- camp conversation;
- a minor site;
- weather;
- a returning NPC;
- one of the heroes noticing how many local stories now involve who can move where.

Foreshadow both **The Silent Tower** and **Ash in the Marsh**:

> Somebody in Candlemere has an old map that does not agree with the roads people currently call inevitable.

> Down south, the Mosslight rumours are starting to sound less like ghost stories and more like a route network somebody depends on.

# Act III — The March Has More Than One Road
## Days 9–12

### Purpose

Deliver the mid-campaign revelation and turn local quest choices into a regional argument.

## Day 9 — Pieces that almost fit

The revelation should become available once the player has encountered **at least two strong evidence threads**, such as:

- the recovered or discussed guild ledger;
- the Moonmere chart / **The Silent Tower**;
- Broken Span's route markings;
- Pilgrim Ford;
- Mosslight's unofficial network;
- ferry inspection patterns.

If the player has avoided Candlemere, the game should still seed the idea through a traveller, camp conversation or copied map note.

## Day 10 — Mid-campaign revelation: the road is a choice

### Core reveal

The Moonmere material shows that the Grey March historically used a **redundant network of roads, ferries, pilgrim ways and seasonal crossings** rather than one fixed hierarchy of official routes.

The current bottlenecks are not purely natural.

Over time:

- toll houses concentrated traffic;
- neglected routes became unofficial routes;
- patrol decisions made some paths “safe” by making others effectively unusable;
- records and charters hardened temporary arrangements into normality;
- the Ashen Veil grew useful precisely where official systems stopped reaching.

The ledger demonstrates who benefits from the current arrangement. The chart demonstrates that the arrangement is not inevitable. Mosslight demonstrates that people have already built alternatives.

### What the reveal is **not**

- not an ancient prophecy;
- not a hidden superweapon;
- not proof that the Archive, Guild, Wardens or Veil caused every crisis;
- not a twist that makes previous local stories irrelevant.

The revelation should make previous quests **more meaningful**, not expose them as distractions.

### Preferred scene

A table in Candlemere, Watcher's Rest or another plausible sheltered place.

A current road map is laid over a copy or rubbing of the Moonmere chart. Several supposedly separate rumours align with old crossings.

Possible Oren line:

> “We keep talking as though the road failed. It did not. We narrowed it until every break became a crisis.”

Possible Mira reply:

> “That sounds better when a scholar says it. Smugglers call it leaving yourself another way out.”

Possible Garrick reply:

> “Another way out is only useful if somebody keeps it passable.”

Possible Brindle reply:

> “And if the people who need it are allowed to use it.”

The party should disagree in a way that previews the final choice without telling the player which answer is correct.

## Day 11 — Four invitations

After the revelation, each faction begins treating the party as potentially useful political capital.

The asks should be concrete.

### Gilt Caravan Guild offer

**Promise:** standardise maintenance, predictable tolls, reliable freight priorities.

**What they ask:** back a single recognised commercial route network with Guild logistics at its centre.

**Real benefit:** fewer supply failures and clearer responsibility.

**Cost:** routes that do not justify themselves commercially become easier to abandon.

### Wardens of the Green March offer

**Promise:** patrol coverage, emergency authority, safer crossings.

**What they ask:** support Warden power to close, inspect or commandeer routes during frontier emergencies.

**Real benefit:** somebody is clearly responsible when lives are immediately at risk.

**Cost:** temporary emergency control can become ordinary control.

### Archive of Candlemere offer

**Promise:** settle competing claims with records, public maps and enforceable obligations.

**What they ask:** place the old route evidence and disputed records into a durable legal framework.

**Real benefit:** power becomes harder to hide or rewrite.

**Cost:** what can be documented may gain priority over needs that exist outside the record.

### Ashen Veil offer

**Promise:** preserve side routes, informal crossings and movement for people official systems do not serve.

**What they ask:** keep control fragmented enough that no institution can close every alternative.

**Real benefit:** access survives when formal routes fail.

**Cost:** essential movement remains dependent on secret brokers, favours and trust that cannot be publicly enforced.

## Day 12 — Party argument, not faction menu

The four heroes should react according to their values and prior memories.

Do not make each hero map neatly onto one faction.

- Garrick values responsibility and may prefer clear duty, but can distrust authority that leaves people undefended.
- Mira values freedom and truth, but should recognise that secrecy can protect predators as easily as vulnerable people.
- Oren values records and responsibility, but his personal history should make him wary of confusing a documented decision with a harmless one.
- Brindle values mercy and faith, but should challenge any “merciful” arrangement that merely moves suffering out of sight.

The player should hear **trade-offs**, not endorsements.

# Act IV — The Cost of Keeping a Route Open
## Days 13–15

### Purpose

Make the revelation materially visible before the finale.

## Day 13 — Factions act before the player decides

The world should change because factions are already responding.

Possible state-dependent examples:

- extra Guild checks around Greyfen if the ledger is missing or hostile;
- Warden patrols around Alderwatch after Mosslight is exposed;
- easier unofficial marsh movement after a brokered Mosslight agreement;
- new Archive attention around Moonmere and old route markers;
- ferry gossip changing after the ledger's fate.

This is where LR-0005's consequence infrastructure should eventually do most of the work.

## Day 14 — People, not abstractions

At least two settlements should show consequences of earlier choices.

Examples:

- Alderwatch remembers whether medicine arrived.
- Hearthwick notices whether caravans returned.
- Candlemere reacts to where the reliquary or ledger ended up.
- Blacksalt remembers whether the player protected or exposed unofficial routes.

The player should be able to say:

> “That is different because of what I did.”

## Day 15 — Hard weather coming

The frontier season hardens.

Do not require a supernatural storm. A stretch of ordinary bad weather is enough:

- rain swells the river;
- Broken Span becomes less trustworthy;
- ferries reduce crossings;
- wagons bunch at bottlenecks;
- Alderwatch starts counting what it can no longer replace.

This should make the final political question urgent:

> The March cannot keep every route equally open for the next few days. Which obligations take priority, and who gets authority to make that call?

# Act V — What the Road Is For
## Days 16–18

### Purpose

Turn the campaign's accumulated local decisions into one difficult regional choice, then show concrete aftermath.

## Day 16 — Convergence at Watcher's Rest

Preferred staging location: **Watcher's Rest**.

Why it works:

- it is a road place rather than a faction capital;
- it has old Warden history without belonging entirely to current authority;
- it feels like a practical frontier meeting, not a royal council;
- it is small enough to keep the tone intimate.

If Watcher's Rest is inaccessible in implementation, Greyfen or another reachable settlement can host the same beat.

Representatives arrive or send messages because the party has become difficult to ignore.

Renown determines **how much weight the party carries**, not which answer is morally available.

### High renown

People expect the party to take a position and can be persuaded to accept a costly compromise.

### Low renown

The factions still make their moves. The party can influence details, but may not be able to bind rivals into a shared arrangement.

This ensures failure to reach 12 renown produces a different political outcome rather than simply a “game over.”

## Day 17 — The hard choice

The final choice should answer:

> **Who should bear responsibility for keeping people moving when the March cannot serve every route equally?**

Five authored approaches:

### 1. Back the Gilt Caravan Guild — **The Gilded Road**

Put logistics, toll collection and route maintenance primarily under Guild coordination.

**Gain:** predictability, freight capacity, supply reliability.

**Cost:** low-profit routes and people without commercial leverage become easier to deprioritise.

### 2. Back the Wardens of the Green March — **The Guarded Road**

Give the Wardens broad emergency authority over crossings, patrols and closures.

**Gain:** rapid response, clearer responsibility, safer main routes.

**Cost:** checkpoints and emergency powers become part of ordinary frontier life.

### 3. Back the Archive of Candlemere — **The Recorded Road**

Use the Moonmere evidence, ledgers and public obligations to build a rules-based route settlement.

**Gain:** transparency, durable evidence, fewer quiet rewrites of responsibility.

**Cost:** slow process and the risk that undocumented need remains politically invisible.

### 4. Back the Ashen Veil — **The Many Roads**

Protect unofficial crossings and prevent any institution from controlling every route.

**Gain:** resilient alternatives and access outside formal systems.

**Cost:** essential infrastructure remains partly dependent on unaccountable brokers and personal trust.

### 5. Refuse a single owner — **The Common Road**

Broker a narrow shared agreement:

- the Guild commits freight and maintenance;
- the Wardens protect crossings but accept explicit limits;
- the Archive publishes route obligations and records decisions;
- unofficial routes remain tolerated when they are carrying genuine need rather than predation.

This is **not** the golden ending.

**Gain:** no faction owns the Grey March's movement outright.

**Cost:** slower coordination, constant negotiation and a fragile compact that will need defending.

The Common Road should require substantial legitimacy and evidence of having worked across faction lines. It should feel earned, not unlocked by choosing “nice” dialogue.

## Day 18 — The March remembers

The ending should not be a single victory paragraph.

It should contain short, specific epilogue blocks:

1. **The road settlement** — which final model was chosen.
2. **Hearthwick / the old road** — bargain or violence, safe or neglected.
3. **Greyfen / the ledger** — who controls or preserves the evidence.
4. **Alderwatch / medicine** — who lived with the timing of the player's choice.
5. **Candlemere / Moonmere / reliquary** — what was preserved and for whom.
6. **Blacksalt / Mosslight** — whether unofficial movement was crushed, documented or negotiated.
7. **The party** — relationship and personal-arc callbacks.
8. **Renown** — whether people tell the story as one where the party shaped the March or merely survived it.

The final sentence should vary by ending family.

No ending should claim the Grey March is permanently fixed.

# Failure, delay and route-freedom rules

## Missing the revelation path

If the player has not recovered the Moonmere chart by the revelation window, do **not** invent the same knowledge from nowhere.

Use a weaker version:

- Candlemere has a partial copy;
- Sen Marrow can show old references but not definitive boundaries;
- an Archive rubbing or ferry record suggests the old network without proving every claim.

Recovering the actual chart later should strengthen the player's leverage in the finale.

## Ignoring the ledger

The campaign still works.

The absence itself becomes political:

- Guild representatives insist the ledger cannot be used against them;
- the Veil claims its disappearance proves official accountability is hollow;
- the Archive argues that unverifiable claims are precisely why durable records matter.

## Failing the medicine run

Do not block the campaign.

Alderwatch becomes harsher evidence of what road failure costs.

The final debate should explicitly prevent the player from treating route politics as abstract after that failure.

## Low renown

Low renown should not remove the final choice screen entirely.

Instead it changes **reach**:

- the player can endorse a faction;
- they may secure one local concession;
- they cannot easily force a broad four-way compact.

The low-renown ending should say that the March heard the party, but did not organise itself around them.

## Unfinished side quests

The ending should name uncertainty where appropriate rather than fabricate closure.

Example:

> Moonmere Tower still leans above the reeds, its old chart either lost, undiscovered or waiting for someone with more time.

# Hero participation in the campaign spine

LR-0044 will author full personal arcs. LR-0043 only defines how the main campaign should leave room for them.

## Garrick Vale

Campaign question for Garrick:

> Is responsibility the same thing as standing in front of everyone else?

Useful finale tension: he should respect clear duty but fear systems where “somebody is responsible” means everyone else stops noticing harm.

## Mira Quickstep

Campaign question for Mira:

> When does freedom become merely leaving the consequences to someone else?

Useful finale tension: she should defend alternative routes while being honest about the people who profit from secrecy.

## Oren Thale

Campaign question for Oren:

> What is a record worth if preserving the truth does not protect the people named in it?

Useful finale tension: the Moonmere chart and ledger should make his Archive instincts personally uncomfortable, not merely validated.

## Sister Brindle

Campaign question for Brindle:

> Can mercy remain mercy when it has to choose who receives scarce help first?

Useful finale tension: the medicine run and route triage should force her to acknowledge that compassion still needs decisions and responsibility.

## Important implementation constraint

The campaign should be driven by **windows and condition checks**, not by forcibly teleporting the player or auto-completing local quests on fixed days.

A player who spends day 10 in the marsh should still experience the revelation, but through a delivery method that makes sense for where they are.

# Tone guardrails for campaign implementation

Use:

- rain, mud, ferries, ledgers, lamps, store rooms, old stone and tired people;
- disagreement between competent adults;
- specific consequences;
- small lines that imply a wider world;
- humour that comes from personality or road weariness.

Avoid:

- “chosen one” framing;
- saving the whole world;
- a secret ancient evil behind ordinary politics;
- faction leaders delivering manifestos;
- modern political slogans;
- a perfect compromise that removes scarcity;
- an ending that treats renown as moral virtue.

The Grey March should end the campaign **changed, argued over and more legible to the player**, not solved forever.
