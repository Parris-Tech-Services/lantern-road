# Lantern Road — Ending Matrix

**Task:** LR-0043  
**Companion to:** `story/campaign/18-DAY-CAMPAIGN-BEAT-SHEET.md`

This document defines the authored ending families and callback matrix for later LR-0008 integration.

It deliberately describes conditions **declaratively**. It does not assign final numeric thresholds or implement state logic.

# Ending construction

Every ending should be assembled from layers rather than selected as one giant prewritten paragraph.

Recommended order:

1. **Road settlement** — the regional political ending family.
2. **Legitimacy** — whether the party had enough renown and cross-region credibility for the decision to carry.
3. **Named quest callbacks** — concrete results from the six current questlines.
4. **Faction aftermath** — who gained trust, access or resentment.
5. **Party aftermath** — character relationships and later personal-arc results.
6. **Final image** — one grounded scene on the road.

This structure makes two players who choose the same faction still receive meaningfully different epilogues.

# Ending families

## Ending A — The Gilded Road
### Final choice: Gilt Caravan Guild

**Core image:** fresh mile posts, repaired wagon ruts and a new toll board before the mud is dry.

### Promise

The Guild can move food, tools, medicine and people at a scale no individual village can match.

### Cost

Routes become easier to justify when they are profitable. A place that cannot pay enough may discover that “reliable logistics” simply means reliable service somewhere else.

### Strong supporting history

Any of these make the ending feel more coherent, but should not be mandatory:

- ledger returned to the Guild;
- strong Gilt Caravan Guild standing;
- medicine delivered successfully;
- false-lantern threat cleared;
- player repeatedly favoured predictable official routes.

### Friction callbacks

The ending should become more uneasy if:

- the ledger went to the Archive or Ashen Veil;
- medicine was diverted;
- Mosslight was brokered rather than exposed;
- Mira has low trust or a freedom-focused personal outcome.

### Final image variants

**High legitimacy:** wagons roll again, and Guild factors publicly credit the party while quietly arguing over the limits the party insisted upon.

**Low legitimacy:** trade improves, but the party's name is mostly attached to one decision inside a settlement the Guild shaped on its own terms.

## Ending B — The Guarded Road
### Final choice: Wardens of the Green March

**Core image:** a lantern at a checkpoint that did not exist two weeks earlier.

### Promise

Someone has clear responsibility when a ferry fails, a road is attacked or medicine needs priority.

### Cost

The same authority that opens a road during an emergency can decide who may use it after the emergency passes.

### Strong supporting history

- false-lantern crew defeated by force;
- medicine delivered;
- Mosslight exposed;
- high Warden standing;
- repeated choices favouring protection, patrols and clear responsibility.

### Friction callbacks

- bargained resolution with the false-lantern crew;
- Mosslight compromise;
- medicine diverted;
- Ashen Veil relationships;
- Garrick may support the duty while questioning unchecked emergency power;
- Brindle may challenge enforcement that becomes punishment.

### Final image variants

**High legitimacy:** the party secures explicit limits on inspections/closures because the Wardens cannot dismiss people the whole March now knows.

**Low legitimacy:** safety improves on the main roads, but the limits come from Warden judgement rather than a compact the party could meaningfully shape.

---

## Ending C — The Recorded Road
### Final choice: Archive of Candlemere

**Core image:** a copied route map pinned where travellers can actually read it, annotations multiplying around the edges.

### Promise

Claims, closures, tolls and obligations become public enough that powerful people cannot simply rewrite yesterday.

### Cost

Records move more slowly than hunger, and people who live outside official records remain easy to overlook.

### Strong supporting history

- Moonmere chart recovered;
- ledger archived as evidence;
- reliquary archived;
- Mosslight evidence recorded;
- high Archive standing.

### Friction callbacks

- lost Moonmere chart;
- ledger returned privately to the Guild or disappeared into Veil routes;
- reliquary returned to the shrine rather than archived;
- Oren may support the method while remembering that a correct record did not prevent his own past harm.

### Final image variants

**High legitimacy:** the Archive publishes obligations that multiple factions reluctantly accept, with explicit room for local testimony and future amendments.

**Low legitimacy:** the Archive preserves the evidence and wins several arguments, but the March still waits for institutions to act on what is now undeniable.

---

## Ending D — The Many Roads
### Final choice: Ashen Veil

**Core image:** a ferry leaves before dawn with no public schedule and exactly the people who needed to be aboard.

### Promise

No authority can close every route. People left outside official systems retain ways to move.

### Cost

Access depends on people whose routes, prices and loyalties cannot be publicly examined.

### Strong supporting history

- ledger given to Nera Vale;
- medicine diverted, especially if later consequences made that choice costly;
- Mosslight brokered;
- strong Ashen Veil standing;
- repeated protection of unofficial crossings.

### Friction callbacks

- Mosslight exposed;
- ledger returned to the Guild;
- strong Warden hostility;
- medicine harm should never be hand-waved because the player later chose the Veil;
- Mira should recognise both the freedom and the danger of invisible power.

### Final image variants

**High legitimacy:** the Veil accepts a few public limits because the party has enough standing to make secrecy answer to witnesses.

**Low legitimacy:** routes stay open for those who know whom to ask, but the party cannot guarantee what happens to people who do not.

---

## Ending E — The Common Road
### Final choice: shared compact / no single owner

**Core image:** four different marks on the same repair notice, with someone already arguing beneath it in charcoal.

### Promise

No faction owns movement outright.

The Guild contributes freight and maintenance. The Wardens protect crossings under defined limits. The Archive records obligations publicly. Unofficial routes remain tolerated when they are carrying genuine need rather than predation.

### Cost

Coordination is slower, compromises are ugly and every future shortage will reopen the argument.

This must **not** be written as the perfect ending.

### Intended eligibility

This ending should require a combination of:

- sufficient renown / regional legitimacy;
- mid-campaign revelation seen with meaningful evidence;
- at least some workable relationships across opposing factions;
- no single faction relationship so catastrophic that cooperation becomes unbelievable;
- evidence that the player has previously accepted trade-offs rather than simply maximising one faction.

Exact numeric thresholds belong to LR-0008/Mechanist-adjacent balancing review, not this authoring task.

### Strong supporting history

Particularly resonant combinations:

- false-lantern threat bargained or resolved without vindictiveness;
- ledger archived as evidence while still preserving practical access;
- medicine delivered;
- reliquary decision treated as a genuine community/institution trade-off;
- Mosslight brokered or documented rather than simply erased;
- mixed but not irreconcilable faction standing.

### Friction callbacks

Every compromise should name at least one dissatisfied person.

Examples:

- Oswin complains that the agreement makes freight planning slower.
- Edda says limits on emergency closures will cost time.
- Holt dislikes tolerated routes that cannot be fully recorded.
- Nera says public rules are already teaching powerful people how to game them.

The final tone is **earned uncertainty**, not triumph.

---

## Ending F — The Fractured March
### Final state: insufficient legitimacy or failed convergence

This is not a punitive “bad ending.” It is the ending where the region changes without accepting the party as the people who could bind its competing interests.

### Typical causes

- renown target missed by a substantial margin;
- major faction relationships deeply hostile;
- critical evidence absent;
- too many unresolved local crises;
- player refuses or cannot sustain a final settlement.

### Result

Each faction acts locally.

- Guild routes become more commercial.
- Warden zones become more controlled.
- Archive claims accumulate.
- Veil alternatives expand where official routes fail.

The March still has roads, but fewer shared assumptions about what they owe one another.

### Final image

A traveller at a fork checking not the weather, but which authority controls each direction.

The party's local good deeds and harms are still remembered. Missing the regional settlement must **not erase quest callbacks**.

# Party callback matrix

The personal-arc task LR-0044 will provide exact lines and outcomes. LR-0043 defines what the ending needs to ask of those arcs.

| Party member | Ending question | Positive callback direction | Tense callback direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Garrick Vale** | Who carries responsibility when authority is shared? | He sees others genuinely taking their share of the burden | He recognises a system that merely found a larger shield to hide behind |
| **Mira Quickstep** | Who gets an escape route, and who controls the secret? | Alternative routes stay open without requiring silence about abuse | “Freedom” becomes another word for power nobody can question |
| **Oren Thale** | Can evidence guide action without becoming an excuse for inaction? | Records are public, revisable and tied to obligations | The Archive wins the argument while people remain exposed |
| **Sister Brindle** | What does mercy owe people when not everyone can be helped first? | The ending names who bears cost and refuses to pretend the choice was painless | Compassion is used as rhetoric while harm is moved somewhere less visible |

Party relationships should affect **tone and epilogue intimacy**, not replace the regional ending family.

A fractured party can achieve a politically successful ending and still end on emotional distance.

A close party can fail to reshape the March and still leave together with a sense of shared purpose.

# Faction-standing use in endings

Faction standing should colour access and tone, not become a hidden morality score.

Recommended interpretation:

- **high positive:** faction representatives trust the party enough to accept criticism or concessions;
- **mild positive:** cordial cooperation;
- **neutral:** outcome depends mainly on concrete quest history;
- **mild negative:** faction complies reluctantly or keeps distance;
- **deep negative:** some ending variants become implausible without extraordinary leverage.

The ending text should always explain **why** a faction behaves as it does using named history where possible.

Bad:

> The Guild dislikes you because standing is -3.

Good:

> Oswin never forgot that the ledger left Guild hands. He signs the agreement because the caravans need it, not because he trusts you.

# Ending eligibility guidance

LR-0008 should eventually implement transparent criteria, but the authored intent is:

## Single-faction endings

Always broadly reachable if that faction is still willing to deal with the player.

The tone varies according to renown and past choices.

## Common Road

Requires **breadth**, not perfection.

Potential declarative requirements:

- campaign revelation seen;
- meaningful evidence from at least two major information threads;
- renown sufficient for regional influence;
- at least three factions not at irreconcilable hostility;
- at least one prior choice demonstrating negotiated or cross-faction problem solving.

No requirement should force the player to choose every “kind” option.

A player who fought the lantern crew, archived the ledger and exposed Mosslight may still broker a Common Road if the rest of the campaign demonstrates enough trust and willingness to limit power.

## Fractured March

Available when no stable regional settlement can credibly form.

It should also remain an explicit refusal option for a player who believes no available structure deserves their endorsement.

# Epilogue assembly examples

These are tone examples, not final runtime copy.

## Common Road + medicine delivered + ledger archived + Mosslight brokered

> The agreement at Watcher's Rest takes three pages, four marks and most of the afternoon. Nobody leaves satisfied. That may be why it survives the week.
>
> Guild wagons carry the first repair timber south. Warden patrols keep the main crossing open under rules Holt insists on posting publicly. Nera's people continue using the reed paths, now with a promise everyone denies having made.
>
> In Alderwatch, Yor still counts every bandage twice. He also has more to count.
>
> The March has not found one road. It has agreed, for now, not to pretend only one matters.

## Guarded Road + medicine failed + Mosslight exposed

> Edda gets the authority she asked for. Within days the main routes are easier to read: patrol markers, inspection points, closure notices.
>
> The improvement is real. So is the line at the checkpoint.
>
> Alderwatch's fever ward never becomes an argument she can win. The road is safer now partly because everyone remembers what happened when nobody could make it move fast enough.
>
> In Blacksalt, several familiar faces stop taking the ferry.

## Many Roads + diverted medicine

> Nera can keep people moving where official roads refuse them. By the end of the week, three crossings the Wardens thought closed are carrying food again.
>
> Alderwatch does not celebrate.
>
> Brindle never lets the party turn the new routes into an excuse for the crate that went elsewhere. The lesson of the March is not that unofficial roads are kinder. It is that every road still has to answer for who it leaves waiting.

## Fractured March + high local success

> Hearthwick has safer wagons. Saint Rhel's lamp burns. Candlemere has the chart. Greyfen has learned to ask harder questions about ledgers.
>
> None of that becomes a shared settlement.
>
> At Redwater Ferry, Vesk keeps four different sets of instructions pinned behind the booth and obeys whichever one is least impossible that morning.
>
> People remember the party kindly in more than one town. The March simply never learned to remember them together.

# Final-line bank

Use sparingly and only where earned.

### Gilded Road

> By winter, the road is easier to price than it is to argue with.

### Guarded Road

> At dusk, more lanterns burn beside the road. Not all of them are there to welcome travellers.

### Recorded Road

> The new map has margins wide enough for corrections. It will need them.

### Many Roads

> The road on the official map ends at the reeds. People keep walking.

### Common Road

> Nobody owns the road. Everybody has work to do.

### Fractured March

> The Grey March still has roads. It no longer agrees where they lead.
