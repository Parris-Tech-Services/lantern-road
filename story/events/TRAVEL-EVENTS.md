# Lantern Road — Travel Events

## T01 — A Funeral Cart Going the Other Way

**Purpose:** consequence reflection; Brindle character; medicine callback.  
**Repeat:** one-shot.  
**Intended conditions:**

- road tile;
- after `Fever on the South Road` has meaningfully progressed or resolved;
- strongest after medicine was late, diverted or failed;
- Brindle present.

### Setup

A narrow cart approaches from Alderwatch under a grey cloth.

The driver raises two fingers in greeting but does not stop.

Brindle does.

Not dramatically. She simply steps aside and bows her head until the cart has passed.

### Branch A — Ask who it was

The driver answers if the road is narrow enough to force a pause.

Do **not** automatically make the death the direct result of the player's medicine choice.

Possible authored line:

> “Old Fenna Mar. Fever took her after forty years of complaining the south road was badly graded.”

Brindle waits until the cart is out of sight.

> “Not every death belongs to us.”

Then:

> “That does not mean none do.”

**Meaning:** prevents simplistic guilt accounting while keeping consequence emotionally present.

### Branch B — Keep moving

Brindle follows after a few steps.

At camp she may later say:

> “Thank you for not asking me to turn a stranger into a lesson.”

**Callback hook:** camp event C06 — *What We Do Not Know*.

### Variant — medicine delivered in time

The cart may instead carry someone who died of an unrelated cause.

The event becomes a reminder that success did not “save Alderwatch” in total.

Brindle:

> “A good outcome is not the same thing as a world without funerals.”

## T02 — Two Maps at the Fork

**Purpose:** foreshadow campaign revelation; Oren/Mira friction.  
**Repeat:** one-shot before campaign revelation.  
**Intended conditions:**

- days 7–11 / obligations-to-revelation phase;
- road fork;
- Oren and Mira present;
- stronger if Moonmere chart not yet recovered.

### Setup

Two nailed boards stand at the fork.

One carries the current Warden/Guild route markings.

The older board behind it has been painted over, but rain has lifted the newer paint in strips.

A second road name is visible underneath.

Oren stops.

Mira does not.

### Branch A — Scrape back more paint

Oren wants to see the older route.

Mira warns that standing beside an official sign with a knife looks exactly like standing beside an official sign with a knife.

If the player proceeds:

> Oren: “This road existed.”
>
> Mira: “Most roads exist. The interesting question is who gets fined for admitting it.”

**Foreshadow:** the current road network is historical choice, not natural inevitability.

### Branch B — Leave the board alone

Mira approves of not advertising interest.

Oren memorises the visible letters anyway.

Later Moonmere-revelation text may acknowledge:

> “The name from the old fork appears on the chart.”

### Branch C — If carrying copied Moonmere evidence

Oren can compare it immediately.

The old board matches.

No reward. The payoff is certainty.

> Oren: “Not theory.”
>
> Mira: “Congratulations. The road is officially real enough to be inconvenient.”

## T03 — The Child Counting Wagons

**Purpose:** show visible old-road consequence without UI abstraction.  
**Repeat:** repeatable once with variant.  
**Intended conditions:**

- near Hearthwick;
- daylight;
- road-safe state either true or false.

### Setup

A child sits on a fence rail with six pebbles in one hand.

Each passing wagon moves one pebble to the other hand.

### Variant A — road still unsafe

The child has moved only one pebble.

> “Mum says six means normal.”

If asked how long they have been counting:

> “Since breakfast.”
>
> It is late afternoon.

### Variant B — road restored

The child has run out of pebbles and started using acorns.

> “Mum says I can stop.”
>
> “I'm winning.”

### Party reactions

**Garrick:** watches the road rather than the child.

**Mira:** adds one acorn to the wrong pile when the child looks away.

**Brindle:** catches her and silently moves it back.

**Oren:** asks what counts as a wagon and is told he is “making the game worse.”

**Meaning:** gives the world a human-scale visual change.

## T04 — A Warden Horse Without a Rider

**Purpose:** tension; responsibility trade-off; Warden standing.  
**Repeat:** one-shot.  
**Intended conditions:**

- hills/plains/road;
- drizzle/rain preferred;
- mid campaign;
- Wardens of the Green March relevant.

### Setup

A saddled Warden horse comes out of the rain alone.

One stirrup is torn.

The animal is not badly hurt.

There is blood on the saddle blanket.

### Choice A — Follow the horse's backtrail immediately

**Trade-off:** lose time and risk walking into whatever happened.

Possible outcomes at integration:

- find injured scout;
- find abandoned gear and signs the scout moved on;
- find nothing definitive.

If Warden standing is low, Garrick can say:

> “Standing does not change who's bleeding.”

### Choice B — Mark the location and continue to the nearest Warden post

**Trade-off:** safer and more efficient, but slower help for the missing rider.

Oren:

> “This is what institutions are for.”

Mira:

> “Provided the institution is where the map says it is.”

### Choice C — If party carries strong medical supplies

The decision text may explicitly acknowledge that they are equipped to intervene.

Do not make carrying medicine force the “correct” branch.

## T05 — Lamp Oil for a Pilgrim

**Purpose:** reliquary callback; small sacrifice; Brindle/Elira thematic echo.  
**Repeat:** one-shot.  
**Intended conditions:**

- road or shrine-adjacent;
- dusk/night;
- carrying lamp oil or enough gold/supplies;
- stronger after reliquary outcome.

### Setup

An elderly pilgrim is relighting the same wick over and over.

The wind keeps taking it.

They are travelling toward Saint Rhel's.

### Variant A — reliquary returned to shrine

> “Heard it's home again.”
>
> The pilgrim shields the wick.
>
> “Figured I should be, too.”

### Variant B — reliquary archived

> “They say it's safe in Candlemere.”
>
> “That's good.”
>
> A beat.
>
> “Still going to the shrine.”

### Choice A — give oil / help relight

No major faction reward.

The pilgrim gives directions to a sheltered path or a small local fact.

**Purpose:** kindness without leverage.

### Choice B — walk with them until the next marker

Costs time.

Brindle may gain a small Trust callback later, but this should not be a hidden morality farm.

### Choice C — continue

The pilgrim does not curse the party.

At most:

> “Road keep you.”

The event should allow refusal without villainising the player.

## T06 — Guild Repair Crew in the Mud

**Purpose:** faction nuance; ledger consequence; show institutions doing real good.  
**Repeat:** repeatable with variant.  
**Intended conditions:**

- road tile;
- near Greyfen/Hearthwick;
- Gilt Caravan Guild standing or ledger outcome affects tone.

### Setup

A Guild crew is laying stone into a section of churned road.

They are soaked, efficient and very obviously improving it.

The foreman recognises the party.

### Variant A — Guild standing positive / ledger returned

> “Factor Marris says you're good for the road.”
>
> “He said it in a longer sentence.”

Crew offers a dry place to stand while a cart passes.

### Variant B — ledger archived / Veil

> “You're the ledger people.”
>
> Nobody stops working.
>
> “Road still needs stone.”

### Choice A — help for half an hour

Potential future effect: road-specific good will / time cost.

Garrick enjoys useful labour more than conversation.

### Choice B — ask who pays for this stretch

The answer is uncomfortable:

> “Guild does.”
>
> “Village pays toll.”
>
> “Warden patrol uses it free.”
>
> “Everybody says that's fair until the bill.”

**Foreshadow:** infrastructure is political because maintenance is real.

### Choice C — move on

No penalty.

The point is to let the player see a faction as more than its abuses.

## T07 — Reed Signs After Rain

**Purpose:** Mosslight consequence; Mira/Nera social memory.  
**Repeat:** callback-only.  
**Intended conditions:**

- swamp;
- after Ash in the Marsh resolves;
- rain/drizzle;
- outcome changes event meaning.

### Setup

Fresh reed bundles have been tied to three branches above the flood line.

Mira recognises a route-sign pattern.

### Variant A — Mosslight brokered

The markers indicate:

> safe crossing / no Warden stores / medicine traffic permitted

Mira:

> “That is either cooperation or an extremely organised lie.”

If Nera relationship is good:

> “She kept the agreement.”

If poor:

> “Or somebody wants us to think she did.”

### Variant B — Mosslight exposed

Two markers are cut down.

The third has a Warden notch carved through it.

> Mira: “That's not a route marker anymore.”
>
> Garrick: “What is it?”
>
> Mira: “A warning about who owns the route.”

### Variant C — Mosslight recorded as evidence

Both route-sign and Warden marks remain.

Oren:

> “Everyone is leaving a record now.”
>
> Mira: “That should terrify you more than it seems to.”

## T08 — The Toll Board Nobody Uses

**Purpose:** make route politics physical; campaign pressure.  
**Repeat:** one-shot.  
**Intended conditions:**

- road;
- days 10–16;
- after at least one faction offer or reputation shift.

### Setup

An old toll board leans sideways.

Four different notices have been nailed over one another:

- Guild rates;
- Warden closure;
- Archive claim notice;
- hand-written detour instructions with no signature.

None agrees on whether the road is open.

### Choice A — follow the newest official notice

Garrick approves of clarity, but asks who posted it.

### Choice B — follow the hand-written detour

Mira asks whether the anonymous writer knew more or merely feared less.

### Choice C — spend time verifying locally

Costs time, yields better information.

Oren:

> “This is what everyone means when they say ‘the road’ as though it were one fact.”

### Later callback

At campaign revelation or convergence, the party can reference:

> “We saw four authorities on one piece of wood.”

## T09 — The Flooded Causeway

**Purpose:** practical trade-off; item/skill condition; party dynamics.  
**Repeat:** one-shot.  
**Intended conditions:**

- rain/storm;
- river-adjacent road;
- rope/tool state changes branches;
- injuries may matter later.

### Setup

Water covers the causeway to ankle depth near the near bank and knee depth beyond.

The current is stronger than it looks.

There is no monster.

That is the point.

### Choice A — cross now with rope

If rope carried:

- Garrick anchors;
- Mira tests footing;
- player chooses who crosses first.

Future injury state may make this choice riskier for a specific member.

### Choice B — wait for the water to fall

Costs meaningful time.

Brindle:

> “Caution is only cheap when nobody is waiting for us.”

### Choice C — take the long road

Costs more time but avoids uncertain hazard.

Oren can note an old high-water marker that hints this route has been unreliable for years.

### Callback variant — Garrick Shared Watch

Garrick automatically assigns roles instead of taking every exposed position.

No dialogue explanation required beyond:

> “Mira tests. I anchor. Oren carries the dry bag.”
>
> He looks at Brindle.
>
> “You tell us if this is foolish.”
>
> Brindle: “You may regret giving me a permanent office.”

## T10 — A Message Meant for Someone Else

**Purpose:** faction pressure; temptation; information ethics.  
**Repeat:** one-shot.  
**Intended conditions:**

- road near Blacksalt/Greyfen;
- mid/late campaign;
- faction standing influences recipient trust.

### Setup

A courier drops a sealed note while mounting in haste.

The outside is addressed to a named local contact the party recognises.

The seal belongs to a faction that may or may not trust the party.

### Choice A — return it unopened

The recipient's reaction depends on faction posture.

No automatic standing gain.

High hostility may produce:

> “You expect thanks?”
>
> “No.”
>
> “Good.”

### Choice B — open it

The message contains **useful but not campaign-breaking** information:

- route closure;
- inspection schedule;
- freight delay;
- meeting relocation.

The important consequence is that the party knows something they were not meant to know.

Later NPC dialogue can detect if that information is used.

### Choice C — leave it where it fell

Mira:

> “A principled commitment to letting somebody else make the bad decision.”

## T11 — Lanterns Behind You

**Purpose:** old-road outcome callback; tension without combat.  
**Repeat:** one-shot callback.  
**Intended conditions:**

- night;
- road;
- after Lanterns on the Old Road resolved.

### Setup

Two lights appear behind the party at the same spacing the false-lantern crew once used.

Nobody speaks for several steps.

### Variant A — bargain outcome

Mira studies them.

> “Could be them.”
>
> Garrick: “Breaking the agreement?”
>
> Mira: “Or obeying it very slowly.”

The lights eventually turn toward a side path.

Later optional discovery: former false-lantern members guiding a legitimate traveller around a washout.

**Meaning:** people can change imperfectly.

### Variant B — fought-clear outcome

Garrick's hand goes to his shield.

The lights turn out to be two ordinary drovers using the same spacing because it is practical on wet roads.

Brindle:

> “Not every lantern behind us is the same night.”

**Meaning:** violence can teach the party to misread ordinary signals.

## T12 — Empty Crates Southbound

**Purpose:** medicine/logistics callback; Joric/Yor themes.  
**Repeat:** one-shot.  
**Intended conditions:**

- road between Greyfen/Alderwatch;
- after medicine quest resolution.

### Setup

A wagon rolls south carrying nothing but empty medical crates being returned for reuse.

The driver knows Yor Dane.

### Variant A — medicine delivered in time

> “He said send the empties back quick.”
>
> “Means he's planning on needing more.”

The good outcome becomes continued obligation, not closure.

### Variant B — medicine late

Some crates still have names chalked on them.

The driver refuses to explain.

### Variant C — medicine diverted

The driver recognises the party.

> “I was supposed to bring these back full.”
>
> “Different road now.”

No confrontation required.

### Choice — ask what Alderwatch needs next

The answer is mundane:

- clean cloth;
- lamp oil;
- dry grain;
- time.

This grounds “saving a settlement” in ongoing work.
