# Leader Background Tags

## Design rule

Each tag answers only:

> **What kind of experience did the Leader have before this campaign that people might reasonably notice?**

It should not answer:

- who the Leader's parents were;
- exactly where they grew up;
- what faction they permanently support;
- whether they are religious;
- what profession they must pursue after the campaign.

The player should still have room to imagine the person behind the tag.

---

# 0. No Stated Background

## Meaning

The player chooses not to define a past category.

This is not a “blank” or inferior option.

It tells the game:

> Let my decisions establish who the Leader is.

## Dialogue use

NPCs do not get special past-knowledge lines.

Instead, more weight falls on remembered behaviour.

Example:

> Tessa: “You never do tell people what you did before the road.”
>
> “Fair enough. You’re doing enough now.”

## Important

No Stated Background must receive the same amount of core story content.

It should never feel like the player forgot to choose the “real” character option.

# 1. Road Hand

## Player-facing description

> **Road Hand** — You have worked enough wagons, ferries, roads or supply lines to know that distance is measured in more than miles.

## What this establishes

The Leader has practical experience around movement/logistics.

It does **not** establish:

- Gilt Caravan Guild membership;
- a specific job;
- a specific town;
- wealth or poverty;
- ownership of a wagon;
- superior combat ability.

## Natural dialogue permissions

The Leader can plausibly recognise:

- overloaded cart balance;
- ordinary freight timing;
- worn rope;
- road-maintenance shortcuts;
- how a small delay multiplies.

Example option:

> **[Road Hand]** “That axle isn’t the problem. The load is.”

## Character implications

### Garrick

Respects practical competence.

### Mira

May joke that Road Hands always know three ways to complain about mud.

### Oren

Appreciates field knowledge he cannot get from documents.

### Brindle

Notices the Leader understands that “logistics” often means somebody waiting.

# 2. Watch-Trained

## Player-facing description

> **Watch-Trained** — You have spent time learning patrol discipline, guard work or frontier emergency procedure.

## What this establishes

The Leader has been trained in some form of organised watch/guard practice.

It does **not** establish:

- current Wardens of the Green March membership;
- officer rank;
- loyalty to Warden policy;
- military service;
- criminal authority.

The ambiguity matters.

A player may have trained under:

- a town watch;
- a caravan guard;
- frontier volunteers;
- a previous Warden posting.

The game should not decide which unless later explicitly chosen.

## Natural dialogue permissions

The Leader can plausibly recognise:

- patrol spacing;
- watch rotations;
- checkpoint procedure;
- poor perimeter discipline;
- when an emergency order lacks basic accountability.

Example:

> **[Watch-Trained]** “Who records the closure order?”

That line can challenge Wardens from inside practical knowledge rather than from abstract politics.

## Character implications

### Garrick

May treat the Leader's judgement as professionally legible, but does not automatically agree.

### Mira

May initially assume the Leader is comfortable with authority until shown otherwise.

### Oren

Likes procedure but may question unwritten custom.

### Brindle

May ask whether the training taught when *not* to give an order.

# 3. Archive-Taught

## Player-facing description

> **Archive-Taught** — You have enough formal learning around records, history or written evidence to recognise how institutions make truth durable.

## What this establishes

The Leader received meaningful education around documents/evidence.

It does **not** establish:

- Archive of Candlemere membership;
- scholarly rank;
- magical training;
- Oren's level of expertise;
- automatic Wits advantage.

## Natural dialogue permissions

The Leader can plausibly recognise:

- seals;
- copyist marks;
- custody language;
- missing citations;
- the difference between a record and an interpretation.

Example:

> **[Archive-Taught]** “Who copied this?”

## Character implications

### Oren

Immediately notices whether the Leader respects evidence or merely likes sounding educated.

### Mira

May test whether the Leader understands that official records can also erase people.

### Garrick

Values useful answers and has little patience for jargon.

### Brindle

May challenge whether preservation is being confused with care.

---

# 4. Pilgrim-Road

## Player-facing description

> **Pilgrim-Road** — You have travelled enough shrine roads, hostels or long communal routes to know how strangers become temporary communities.

## What this establishes

The Leader has meaningful experience travelling routes shaped by hospitality, shrines and shared custom.

It does **not** establish:

- a specific faith;
- priesthood;
- devotion to Saint Rhel;
- Brindle's tradition;
- pacifism;
- fixed moral virtue.

The player can have travelled as:

- believer;
- companion;
- porter;
- curious traveller;
- someone seeking shelter.

## Natural dialogue permissions

The Leader can plausibly recognise:

- pilgrim marks;
- hospitality customs;
- ordinary shrine etiquette;
- when sacred spaces are functioning as road infrastructure;
- how travellers share risk.

Example:

> **[Pilgrim-Road]** “A shrine lamp is also a road marker.”

## Character implications

### Brindle

May ask what the road meant to the Leader rather than assuming faith.

### Oren

Interested in oral/customary knowledge.

### Mira

Knows pilgrim routes often overlap with unofficial routes.

### Garrick

Respects the practical community aspect more than religious vocabulary.

# Why these four

They each connect to an existing campaign pillar without assigning the Leader to a faction.

| Background | Main lens | Faction overlap without membership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Road Hand | logistics / travel | Guild |
| Watch-Trained | responsibility / safety | Wardens |
| Archive-Taught | evidence / records | Archive |
| Pilgrim-Road | community / informal route custom | shrine roads / overlaps Veil routes |

The Ashen Veil deliberately does **not** receive a “former smuggler” background.

That would over-specify criminal history and create a stronger faction tie than the light-identity contract needs.

---

# Background use limits

## One line, not a whole alternate game

A background option should appear where it adds perspective.

Do not prefix every conversation with a background response.

Aim for:

- a few early recognition moments;
- one or two strong quest uses;
- several reactive callbacks;
- occasional flavour.

## No automatic correctness

Background knowledge can still be outdated.

Road Hand:

> “That rope should hold.”

Mira:

> “Should is doing dangerous work there.”

Archive-Taught:

> “That seal is genuine.”

Oren:

> “The seal is genuine. The copy may not be.”

This keeps companions useful.

## No compulsory exposition

Do not make background dialogue the only way to learn critical quest facts.

It is an alternate lens, not a hidden difficulty setting.
