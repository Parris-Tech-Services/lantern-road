# Background-Sensitive Reactions

These are representative authored reactions.

They demonstrate how companions and NPCs can recognise the Leader's background without reducing every conversation to a tag check.

---

# Garrick Vale

## Leader: Road Hand

After the Leader corrects a badly loaded cart:

> Garrick watches the axle settle.
>
> “You've done that before.”
>
> Leader may answer:
>
> - “Enough times.”
> - “Enough times to hate it.”
> - “Enough times to know we're still doing it slowly.”

Later:

> Garrick asks the Leader to inspect a strap before travel without making a speech about trust.

## Leader: Watch-Trained

After the Leader challenges a poor watch rotation:

> “You count posts like somebody taught you to.”
>
> Garrick does not sound suspicious.
>
> He sounds interested.

If the Leader later ignores procedure for a good reason:

> “Training says one thing.”
>
> “You chose another.”
>
> “Tell me why.”

## Leader: Archive-Taught

Garrick after a long evidence discussion:

> “You and Oren can both read the same page and still find two arguments.”
>
> “Useful.”
>
> “Terrifying.”

## Leader: Pilgrim-Road

At Saint Rhel's:

> Garrick notices the Leader checking the lamp niche before the altar.
>
> “Road habit?”
>
> If yes:
>
> “Makes more sense to me than most holy habits.”

# Mira Quickstep

## Leader: Road Hand

> “Road Hand.”
>
> Mira taps a muddy wheel rut.
>
> “Tell me whether that's a shortcut or a confession.”

Later, if the Leader makes a good route call:

> “Annoying.”
>
> “You actually do know mud.”

## Leader: Watch-Trained

Early:

> “Watch-trained?”
>
> Mira's expression sharpens.
>
> “Should I start hiding things now or wait until you ask politely?”

This is teasing with a real test underneath.

If the Leader repeatedly protects private information:

> “You know, you're very disappointing for someone with guard habits.”

## Leader: Archive-Taught

> “You can read seals?”
>
> “Wonderful.”
>
> “Now I have two people capable of explaining why paperwork is dangerous.”

If Leader later protects a secret:

> “Turns out literacy doesn't always end in filing.”

## Leader: Pilgrim-Road

Mira sees the Leader recognise a route mark:

> “Pilgrim?”
>
> “That explains why you keep finding roads the toll collectors forgot.”

She may later ask about shared shelters rather than faith.

# Oren Thale

## Leader: Road Hand

Oren describes a route based on a historical map.

Leader:

> **[Road Hand]** “That grade won't take a loaded wagon.”

Oren stops.

> “No.”
>
> “You're right.”
>
> He writes it down.

Later:

> “I need a practical objection.”
>
> He looks at the Leader.
>
> “Please disappoint my theory.”

## Leader: Watch-Trained

> “You keep asking who signed the order.”
>
> Oren smiles.
>
> “I keep asking who preserved it.”
>
> “Between us, bureaucracy may finally become useful.”

## Leader: Archive-Taught

Oren tests rather than assumes kinship:

> “Archive-taught where?”
>
> Leader option:
>
> - “Enough to recognise a trap disguised as a footnote.”
> - “Enough to know you're about to correct me.”
> - “I'd rather not say.”

If the Leader avoids answering, Oren respects the boundary.

## Leader: Pilgrim-Road

> “Pilgrim routes preserve knowledge very differently.”
>
> Oren catches himself.
>
> “Sorry.”
>
> “That was almost a lecture invitation disguised as a question.”

# Sister Brindle

## Leader: Road Hand

When the party discusses medicine timing:

> “You understand this already.”
>
> Brindle looks at the road.
>
> “A delay is never only a delay to the person waiting.”

## Leader: Watch-Trained

> “Training teaches you to decide quickly?”
>
> Brindle asks.
>
> “Did anyone teach what to do with the people who disagree afterward?”

This can become a recurring leadership theme.

## Leader: Archive-Taught

At the reliquary:

> “You know what Candlemere can preserve.”
>
> Brindle says.
>
> “Good.”
>
> “Then you also know preservation is an argument, not an answer.”

## Leader: Pilgrim-Road

Brindle does not assume shared belief.

> “What did pilgrim roads mean to you?”
>
> Possible answers:
>
> - “Shelter.”
> - “People.”
> - “Faith.”
> - “A cheaper bed than an inn.”
>
> Brindle accepts all four as real answers.

# Tessa Reed

## Any background

Tessa should be one of the NPCs least impressed by labels.

### Road Hand

> “Road Hand?”
>
> “Then stack those mugs like cargo and prove it.”

### Watch-Trained

> “Watch-trained?”
>
> “Wonderful.”
>
> “Keep an eye on table three. They're cheating badly.”

### Archive-Taught

> “Archive-taught?”
>
> She hands over a slate.
>
> “Tell me whether ‘paid next week’ counts as fiction.”

### Pilgrim-Road

> “Pilgrim roads teach patience.”
>
> “This room teaches the opposite.”

**Purpose:** prevents background tags from feeling like heroic titles.

# Oswin Marris

## Road Hand

> “At least somebody in this conversation understands that freight is not moved by moral enthusiasm.”

Leader can challenge:

> “Neither is it moved by pretending delayed roads don't cost people.”

## Watch-Trained

> “Guard training.”
>
> Oswin smiles.
>
> “Then you already know every inspection is vital until your cart is the one waiting.”

## Archive-Taught

Oswin becomes more careful with words.

> “You know enough to be inconvenient.”

## Pilgrim-Road

> “Pilgrim routes are admirable.”
>
> “Terrible freight capacity.”

This is an opportunity for the Leader to challenge what counts as value.

# Halden Crow

## Road Hand

> “Tell me which closure actually hurts traffic.”
>
> Crow asks it as a real question.

## Watch-Trained

Crow immediately asks:

> “Who trained you?”

The player can refuse the question.

Crow:

> “Fair.”
>
> “I asked because half of bad procedure is people copying someone they can't name.”

## Archive-Taught

> “Good.”
>
> “Write down exactly what I said so Marris can't improve it later.”

## Pilgrim-Road

Crow:

> “Pilgrim ways make patrol maps messy.”
>
> Leader:
>
> “People don't travel for the convenience of patrol maps.”

Crow may respect the answer even while disagreeing.
