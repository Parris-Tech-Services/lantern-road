# Emergent Leadership Behaviour Callbacks

## Principle

The Leader's real identity should emerge more from **remembered behaviour** than from the chosen background.

Do not expose visible alignment axes such as:

- Diplomatic +4;
- Harsh +2;
- Secretive 70%.

Instead, record selected meaningful memories and let characters describe the pattern naturally.

---

# Pattern A — Keeps promises

## Trigger intent

The Leader makes a specific promise and later fulfils it.

Examples:

- clears the old road after promising Rowan;
- does not reveal a protected name;
- returns for someone;
- delivers a promised item without redirecting it.

## Companion callbacks

### Garrick

> “You said we'd come back.”
>
> “We did.”
>
> He makes it sound like a tactical fact.
>
> It is praise.

### Mira

> “You know people are going to start believing you when you say things.”

### Oren

> “Repeated reliability is evidence.”
>
> Mira:
>
> “He means he likes you.”

### Brindle

> “A kept promise does not fix everything.”
>
> “It does make the next promise less lonely.”

## NPC callback

Rowan:

> “You don't promise often.”
>
> “Good habit.”

# Pattern B — Breaks or over-promises

## Trigger intent

The Leader makes specific promises and fails them without renegotiating.

This should not trigger from unavoidable failure where the Leader honestly returned and explained.

## Companion callbacks

### Garrick

> “Say what we're doing.”
>
> “Not what you hope will be true afterward.”

### Mira

> “Maybe stop giving people certainty as a gift.”
>
> “It keeps turning into debt.”

### Oren

> “Intent is not the same thing as an accurate forecast.”

### Brindle

> “An apology is better than pretending the promise changed by itself.”

## NPC callback

Rowan:

> “Tell me what you intend.”
>
> “Leave promises out of it.”

# Pattern C — Consults companions

## Trigger intent

Leader repeatedly asks companions for judgement before major but still Leader-owned decisions.

This does not mean always following their advice.

## Garrick

> “You ask.”
>
> “Then you decide.”
>
> “I can work with that.”

## Mira

> “I appreciate being consulted before we do the dangerous thing.”
>
> “It's very modern.”

## Oren

> “You keep requesting evidence and then making your own decisions.”
>
> “Disturbingly healthy.”

## Brindle

> “You don't make responsibility smaller by sharing the thinking.”

## Reserve companion callback

When rotated back in:

> “Garrick said you've started asking before deciding.”
>
> Mira, if she is the returning companion:
>
> “I assume he made it sound less irritating.”

The reserve companion can know the **public pattern** through party debrief, not private conversations they did not witness.

# Pattern D — Commands frequently

## Trigger intent

Leader repeatedly chooses COMMAND where request/suggestion was available.

Do not judge automatically.

In crisis this may be appropriate.

The callback should reflect frequency/context.

## Garrick

If commands were decisive and useful:

> “You give orders cleanly.”
>
> “Just remember people still have to live inside them afterward.”

If commands were personal/micromanaging:

> “You don't need to order me to do what I already agreed to.”

## Mira

> “You know the difference between leading and narrating everybody's hands, right?”

## Oren

> “I respond better to reasons.”
>
> “You have noticed.”
>
> “I am choosing to assume you have noticed.”

## Brindle

> “Authority is useful.”
>
> “So is leaving people enough room to mean yes.”

# Pattern E — Admits uncertainty honestly

## Trigger intent

Leader uses honest uncertainty in moments where bluffing certainty was available.

## Garrick

> “You said you didn't know.”
>
> “Then we planned for both answers.”
>
> “That worked.”

## Mira

> “Very brave.”
>
> “Admitting you have no idea in front of people who expect a plan.”

## Oren

> “Uncertainty stated accurately is information.”

## Brindle

> “People can carry uncertainty.”
>
> “What exhausts them is pretending it isn't there.”

## NPC callback

Edda:

> “You don't guess when a patrol can hear you.”
>
> “Keep that habit.”

# Pattern F — Protects secrets accepted in confidence

## Trigger intent

Leader is entrusted with a personal/faction-sensitive secret and repeatedly refuses opportunities to reveal it.

## Mira

> “You keep things.”
>
> She pauses.
>
> “Not everything.”
>
> “The things you agreed to keep.”

That distinction matters.

## Oren

> “A record withheld can be ethical.”
>
> “Please never tell Holt I said that without context.”

## Brindle

> “Privacy is not the same as deception.”

## Nera

> “You are becoming inconveniently difficult to buy.”

# Pattern G — Reveals secrets when stakes change

Protecting every secret forever should not be treated as virtue.

If the Leader reveals information because someone is at immediate risk:

### Mira

If consulted first:

> “I hate it.”
>
> “I also know why.”

If not consulted:

> “You made my boundary part of your calculation without telling me.”

### Garrick

> “If keeping it would get somebody hurt, say that plainly.”

### Brindle

> “Sometimes confidence stops being private when another person's life is inside it.”

**Purpose:** avoid building a hidden “secretive good / revealing bad” morality axis.

# Pattern H — Faces consequences directly

## Trigger intent

After a hard outcome, Leader returns to the people affected rather than avoiding them.

Examples:

- returns to Alderwatch after medicine failure/diversion;
- tells Elira where the reliquary went;
- tells Edda they withheld Mosslight names;
- tells Oswin directly the ledger went elsewhere.

## Garrick

> “You went back.”
>
> “Good.”

## Mira

> “Most people get very busy after disappointing someone.”

## Oren

> “Reporting a result is part of the decision.”

## Brindle

> “You let them be angry at the right person.”

This can be one of the strongest emergent Leader identity markers.

---

# Pattern memory limits

Do not infer a permanent personality from one action.

Recommended threshold:

- one event = specific memory;
- repeated similar actions = pattern callback becomes eligible;
- contradictory later action can change the tone.

Example:

A Leader who usually consults companions but suddenly commands in a crisis should get:

> “You don't normally order people.”
>
> Garrick tightens his grip on the shield.
>
> “So I'm listening.”

This is more meaningful than a static trait.
