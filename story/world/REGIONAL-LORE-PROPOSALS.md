# Proposed Grey March Regional Lore

**Status:** Storyteller proposal for LR-0071.  
**Not canon until Agent 7 ratifies it under the map-canon protocol.**

The aim is to make existing terrain easier to remember, not to enlarge the campaign.

These regional names should appear sparingly in:

- map overlay labels;
- rumours;
- travel descriptions;
- NPC directions;
- campaign prose.

They are not additional destinations.

---

# Hollowwold

## Proposed status

**ADOPT**

## What locals mean

The **Hollowwold** is the rough northern interior around Hollowglass Cavern: broken upland, wind channels, shallow folds in the ground and stretches where the stone seems to carry sound farther than it should.

The name does not imply the land is empty.

It comes from the way wind moves through cracks, sinkholes and mineral seams.

### Local saying

> “If the Hollowwold goes quiet, check the weather.”

People who know the country listen for changes in its ordinary ringing and rustling.

## Canon relationships

- **Hollowglass Cavern** is the strongest anchor.
- **Watcher's Rest** sits west of the Hollowwold approaches rather than inside a newly invented settlement network.
- It provides plausible landscape context for old Warden weather logs and northern route knowledge.

## Campaign use

Useful for:

- weather foreshadowing;
- Oren's geological curiosity;
- travel directions that feel more local than “go north”;
- distinguishing upland travel from central road country.

Do not add Hollowwold villages, factions or quest hubs without separate approval.

---

# Reedmarsh

## Proposed status

**ADOPT**

## What locals mean

The **Reedmarsh** is the low southern country where river, flooded track, reed path and unofficial crossing stop behaving like separate systems.

A road there can become water after rain.

A path can become a ferry agreement.

A ferry agreement can become a favour.

Locals speak of “the Reedmarsh” without agreeing exactly where it begins.

That ambiguity is useful and believable.

## Canon relationships

The name can naturally encompass the existing southern network around:

- **Pilgrim Ford**;
- **Redwater Ferry**;
- **Blacksalt Crossing**;
- **Smuggler's Cache**;
- **Mosslight Ruins**;
- existing southern swamp/river terrain.

It does not relocate any of them.

## Human identity

People in Greyfen may talk about “sending something into the Reedmarsh.”

People in Blacksalt are more likely to say:

> “You mean home?”

Wardens may use the term operationally.

The Ashen Veil may dislike outsiders speaking as though one word means they understand every reed path.

## Campaign use

This should become one of the most useful regional names because the core campaign is already about who controls movement there.

It supports:

- Mosslight;
- medicine routes;
- ferry inspection;
- ledger movement;
- hidden crossings;
- the final argument about multiple roads.

## Tone

The Reedmarsh is not cursed wilderness.

It is inhabited, worked, navigated and politically complicated.

# Barrow Ridge

## Proposed status

**RENAME replacement for Ashen Ridge**

## What locals mean

**Barrow Ridge** is the stony rise around Old Barrow Keep and the broken high ground that makes the ruin visible before it is reachable.

The name predates anyone's current faction claim.

It refers to old earthworks and burials in the ridge country, not to a new kingdom or culture.

## Canon relationships

- anchored by **Old Barrow Keep**;
- can sit between central road country and the eastern approaches;
- may provide a geographic reason older routes bend around higher ground.

## Campaign use

Good for:

- directions;
- old-road history;
- weather visibility;
- rumours about Barrow Keep.

Avoid turning every hill near the keep into archaeological content.

---

# Watcherwood

## Proposed status

**RENAME replacement for Duskwood**

## What locals mean

**Watcherwood** is the wooded country on the western/north-western approaches, named from generations of travellers using Watcher's Rest as the last reliable roof before taking the smaller tracks.

The name may be newer than the trees.

That is fine.

Most useful landscape names are.

## Canon relationships

- strongest anchor: **Watcher's Rest**;
- complements existing forest terrain west/north-west of the main road;
- **Saint Rhel's Shrine** can lie on the broader southern pine-country edge without being absorbed into a new location.

## Campaign use

Good for:

- old Warden routes;
- weather logs;
- travellers choosing between road and wooded track;
- early-game atmosphere around Watcher's Rest.

Do not invent a “Watcherwood people” culture.

---

# Greyfen Plain

## Proposed status

**RENAME replacement for Blackfen Plains**

## What locals mean

The **Greyfen Plain** is the central open road country around Greyfen Market.

It is not featureless.

Its defining feature is visibility:

- carts can see weather coming;
- patrols can see smoke;
- travellers can see the next road marker;
- people can also see when traffic stops.

## Canon relationships

- **Greyfen Market** is its obvious social/economic anchor;
- the main east-west road runs through this broader open country;
- **Broken Span** sits toward the southern/western pressure of the central route network.

## Campaign use

This name helps explain why Greyfen became a market:

> open approach + route convergence + enough dry ground to argue about tolls on.

It supports the Guild/Warden/ledger material without adding a new town.

---

# Stoneveil Heights

## Proposed status

**RENAME replacement for Stoneveil Mountains**

## What locals mean

The **Stoneveil Heights** are the northern high-country edge of the Grey March: mountain backs, steep hills and pale rock that often disappear into low cloud.

From the playable road country they are more often a horizon and weather source than a destination.

## Canon relationships

- corresponds conceptually to existing northern mountain/hill terrain;
- provides a geographic source for cold wind and shifting weather;
- does **not** establish a new mountain settlement or travel region.

## Campaign use

Useful in:

- weather descriptions;
- map orientation;
- Watcher's Rest logs;
- Oren/Archive historical references.

### Example

> Rain had already hidden the Stoneveil Heights by breakfast.

This makes the world feel wider without adding another map.

# Rejected labels

## Embermere

No replacement proposed.

A second “mere” identity is unnecessary while Moonmere already carries campaign significance.

## Siltbrook Marsh

No replacement proposed.

Reedmarsh plus Redwater already provides enough naming for the southern wet country.

# Suggested NPC usage

Regional names should sound natural, not like every NPC memorised a gazetteer.

## Rowan Pike

Likely to say:

> “Weather's coming down off the Stoneveil Heights.”

Not:

> “Travel north into the officially designated Stoneveil Heights region.”

## Tessa Reed

> “Two carts came through the Watcherwood road before dark.”

## Joric Pell

> “Once the crate hits the Reedmarsh, hours stop behaving.”

## Sen Marrow

> “The Moonmere chart uses three older names for what we now call the Greyfen Plain.”

## Edda Briar

> “Reedmarsh route. Two scouts. No heroics.”

## Nera Vale

> “People from Greyfen say Reedmarsh when they mean the part they can draw.”

These differences help the region feel lived in.

---

# Visual-label guidance for Agent 4 / Agent 7

If ratified:

- prefer **fewer, stronger regional labels**;
- labels must sit beneath canonical settlement/site labels in hierarchy;
- do not make regional names look like new clickable destinations;
- do not add roads, water bodies or settlement symbols to justify a generated name;
- if a proposed label cannot fit the existing terrain cleanly, drop the label rather than moving canon.

The map should help the player learn the Grey March, not convince them there is a second game outside the borders.
