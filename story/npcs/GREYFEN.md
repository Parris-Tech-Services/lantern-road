# Greyfen Market — Recurring NPC Dialogue

Greyfen should feel like the settlement where every moral problem eventually acquires an invoice.

Its three voices:

- **Oswin Marris** turns risk into leverage.
- **Halden Crow** turns ambiguity into a security problem.
- **Joric Pell** turns delays into practical consequences.

---

# Oswin Marris
**Role:** Guild Factor  
**Faction:** Gilt Caravan Guild

## Voice

Polished, economical and never accidentally candid.

When Oswin is angry, he becomes more courteous.

## Motive

Keep the Gilt Caravan Guild indispensable without admitting how much that requires other people to accept Guild priorities.

## Callback A — Ledger returned to the Guild

> “I was beginning to worry you had developed a taste for dramatic evidence.”
>
> Oswin closes the ledger cabinet.
>
> “You chose the boring ending.”
>
> A pause.
>
> “Civilisation is mostly boring endings.”

**Repeat line**

> “The relevant accounts are considerably less exciting now.”
>
> “Thank you.”

## Callback B — Ledger archived as evidence

> “Holt has a copy of my headaches with numbered tabs.”
>
> Oswin smiles.
>
> “I admire the Archive's efficiency.”
>
> “At making embarrassment durable.”

**Repeat line**

> “I assume Candlemere is keeping the paper dry.”
>
> “One of us should.”

## Callback C — Ledger given to the Ashen Veil

> Oswin's smile appears exactly on time.
>
> “I understand you entrusted commercial records to people whose central business practice is avoiding records.”
>
> “Bold.”
>
> “Please do not mistake that word for praise.”

**Repeat line**

> “If Nera ever offers you a receipt, frame it.”

## Callback D — high Guild standing

> “You have become inconveniently credible.”
>
> Oswin taps a shipping slate.
>
> “It means when you criticise us, people ask whether you are right instead of whether you are bitter.”
>
> “I preferred the second problem.”

## Callback E — Common Road / shared-responsibility campaign direction

**Condition intent**

- late campaign;
- after faction offers are known;
- does not restate the final campaign choice.

> “Shared responsibility is a phrase people enjoy until an axle breaks.”
>
> Oswin folds his hands.
>
> “Then they look for one person to send the bill to.”
>
> “If you want four institutions responsible, decide who signs when the rain starts.”

**Purpose**

Adds practical pressure without becoming a manifesto.

# Halden Crow
**Role:** Guard Captain  
**Faction:** Wardens of the Green March

## Voice

Blunt, suspicious and more thoughtful than his bluntness initially suggests.

Crow dislikes cleverness when cleverness hides who gets hurt.

## Motive

Keep routes safe enough that ordinary people do not have to become experts in danger.

## Callback A — False-lantern crew bargained away

> Crow listens to the report twice.
>
> “So they're alive.”
>
> “The road is open.”
>
> “And you think the deal will hold.”
>
> He rubs his jaw.
>
> “I dislike every part of that sentence except the middle one.”

**Repeat line**

> “No bodies. No lanterns.”
>
> “I can live with being annoyed.”

## Callback B — False-lantern crew fought clear

> “You did what I would have done.”
>
> Crow says it like a warning.
>
> “Do not take that as proof it was the only answer.”

**Repeat line**

> “Road's clear.”
>
> “Keep watching the people who learned force works.”

## Callback C — high Warden standing

> “My scouts stop arguing when I say you saw something.”
>
> Crow grimaces.
>
> “That's useful.”
>
> “It's also how institutions get lazy, so keep bringing evidence.”

## Callback D — low Warden standing

> “You're not under arrest.”
>
> Crow waits.
>
> “You looked disappointed.”
>
> “I can make the conversation longer if you need the drama.”

Then, serious:

> “I don't trust your judgement right now.”
>
> “That does not mean I stop listening.”

## Callback E — Garrick Held Line outcome

> Crow nods at Garrick.
>
> “Heard you stayed on that crossing until your boots filled.”
>
> Garrick: “News travels.”
>
> Crow: “So does the habit of making yourself indispensable.”
>
> Garrick does not answer.

## Callback F — Garrick Shared Watch outcome

> “Training locals?”
>
> Crow looks mildly offended.
>
> “Careful, Vale. If people learn to solve things before we arrive, the Wardens may have to develop personalities.”

# Joric Pell
**Role:** Caravan Master  
**Faction:** Gilt Caravan Guild

## Voice

Weather-beaten, impatient with theory and unexpectedly tender about logistics.

Joric measures choices in hours, wheels, feed and people waiting at the other end.

## Motive

Make sure things that need moving actually move.

## Callback A — Medicine delivered in time

> “Yor sent word.”
>
> Joric checks the straps on an empty wagon.
>
> “Said the crate arrived before he had to start choosing which shelf stayed empty.”
>
> He looks at you.
>
> “That's what being on time means out here.”

**Repeat line**

> “Alderwatch still owes me a crate.”
>
> “That's a good kind of debt.”

## Callback B — Medicine late but useful

> “Late isn't the same as useless.”
>
> Joric says it before you can.
>
> “But don't let anybody turn that into ‘close enough.’”
>
> “People at the end of a road can tell the difference.”

## Callback C — Medicine diverted

> Joric does not raise his voice.
>
> “I gave you a sealed crate because I couldn't be in two places.”
>
> “You made it mean I wasn't in either.”

**Repeat line**

> “I have work to do.”
>
> It is not dismissal.
>
> It is exactly what he says when he has decided anger cannot move freight.

## Callback D — strong Guild standing after medicine success

> “Oswin likes numbers.”
>
> “Tell him this one: six hours saved on a wet road is worth more than ten percent off lamp oil.”

**Purpose**

Keeps economy consequences human.

## Callback E — Brindle scout-allocation outcome

> Joric listens to Brindle explain why they chose the scout.
>
> “People hate admitting that saving one person can save a road.”
>
> “They hate even more admitting the road doesn't make that person worth more.”
>
> Brindle: “Yes.”
>
> Joric: “Good. Then we agree on the difficult part.”
