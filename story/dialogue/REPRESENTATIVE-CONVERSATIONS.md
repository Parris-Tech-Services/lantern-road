# Representative Player-Leader Conversations

These are authoring exemplars, not final runtime scripts.

They demonstrate how the Leader, companion autonomy and memory should feel.

---

# 1. Garrick — The Bandage

**Context:** Garrick is injured after protecting another party member.  
**Purpose:** show suggestion, refusal, command and delayed memory.

Garrick is retying the strap on his shield with one hand.

You hold out a bandage.

> **Leader choice A — “Take it. We have enough.”**

Garrick:

> “Enough is a dangerous word.”

If Trust high:

> He takes it anyway.
>
> “But I trust your count.”

**Memory intent:** Leader asked directly and Garrick accepted help.

Later callback:

> At the next supply check Garrick hands you the bandage count first.
>
> “You're apparently in charge of defining enough.”

---

> **Leader choice B — “You took that hit for Mira. Let her decide whether the bandage was wasted.”**

Mira:

> “Oh, excellent.”
>
> “I vote against dramatic bleeding.”

Garrick looks at her, then takes the bandage.

**Consequence:** strengthens Garrick–Mira bond if current relationship allows humour.

Delayed callback:

> Mira later tosses Garrick a clean wrap.
>
> “Democracy.”

---

> **Leader choice C — “If you say you can keep moving, I believe you.”**

Garrick:

> “I can.”

He does not take it.

**Trade-off:** respects autonomy but can reinforce his flaw.

Delayed callback:

> Later, Garrick is slower climbing a bank.
>
> He notices you noticing.
>
> “You trusted my answer.”
>
> It is not accusation.
>
> It is not entirely gratitude either.

---

> **Leader choice D — “No. That’s an order.”**

High Trust:

> Garrick's jaw tightens.
>
> He takes the bandage.
>
> “Then own the order.”
>
> “If someone else needs this later, we both remember who decided.”

Low Trust:

> “You can order where I stand in a fight.”
>
> “You don't get to order me to pretend this is the best use of supplies.”

Possible hard refusal.

# 2. Mira — Ask About the Old Name

**Context:** `mira_old_name` memory exists.  
**Purpose:** demonstrate topic-state memory and respecting/refusing boundaries.

First deeper conversation:

> **Leader — “You said people on that road knew another name.”**

Mira:

> “I did.”
>
> “Very observant.”

### Choice A — “You don’t have to tell me.”

> Mira studies you.
>
> “That sounded suspiciously like patience.”
>
> “Careful.”

**Memory:** Leader did not press.

Repeat conversation should NOT offer:

> “What was your old name?”

Instead:

> **“I said I wouldn’t press. I haven’t.”**

Mira:

> “I noticed.”

Delayed callback when someone later calls her Venn:

> Mira looks at you before anyone else.
>
> “You waited.”
>
> “So I'll tell you now.”

---

### Choice B — “I need to know if it can put the party at risk.”

Mira loses some humour.

> “That's fair.”

She explains risk without necessarily giving the name.

**Memory:** Leader framed personal secret as operational risk.

Delayed callback:

> “You asked whether my past endangered everyone.”
>
> “Today it might.”
>
> “So this time I'm telling you before you ask.”

---

### Choice C — “Everyone else has trusted me with something. Your turn.”

Mira:

> “That is not how trust works.”

High Trust may open a difficult conversation.

Low Trust closes topic.

**Memory:** Leader pressured Mira by comparing vulnerability.

Delayed callback:

> When another party member opens up, Mira says:
>
> “Nobody owes the fire a confession because somebody else went first.”

# 3. Oren — Correct the Leader

**Context:** party has partial Moonmere evidence.

Leader proposes:

> “We tell Crow the old chart proves the eastern crossing is safe.”

Oren:

> “It proves the eastern crossing was used.”
>
> “Those are different sentences.”

### Choice A — “Then give me the sentence you can defend.”

Oren pauses.

> “The chart supports investigating the eastern crossing as a credible alternative route.”

Mira:

> “A sentence only an archivist could pack for travel.”

**Memory:** Leader accepted correction.

Delayed callback:

Later, when Oren overstates something:

> **Leader option:** “Give me the sentence you can defend.”

Oren recognises his own phrase and smiles despite himself.

---

### Choice B — “Crow needs clarity, not a lecture.”

Oren:

> “Clarity built from missing facts is usually called confidence.”

He may still agree to simplify:

> “Say the chart gives us reason to inspect it.”
>
> “Do not say safe.”

**Consequence:** mild friction, no automatic Trust loss if Leader accepts boundary.

---

### Choice C — “Just tell him it’s safe.”

High Trust:

> “No.”
>
> Oren looks uncomfortable but does not soften it.
>
> “I will not put my name behind that statement.”

Leader can:

- say it personally;
- back down;
- ask Mira;
- argue further.

**Important:** high Trust makes refusal clearer, not impossible.

Delayed callback:

If crossing later proves safe:

> Oren:
>
> “For the record, being lucky did not make the earlier claim evidence.”

# 4. Brindle — Comfort Without False Certainty

**Context:** frightened travellers ask whether a dangerous route is protected by the Lamp.

Leader:

> “Brindle, tell them the Lamp will keep them safe.”

Brindle looks at you.

> “I won't.”

### Choice A — “What can you honestly tell them?”

> “That they're frightened.”
>
> “That we can walk with them as far as the marker.”
>
> “That fear is not proof the road is wrong.”
>
> “And that I do not know what waits after the bend.”

Travellers can choose with honest support.

**Memory:** Leader accepted Brindle's spiritual boundary.

Delayed callback:

> Brindle later says:
>
> “Thank you for asking what I could say instead of making refusal the end of the conversation.”

---

### Choice B — “They need hope.”

> “Yes.”
>
> “Hope and certainty are not the same service.”

This can deepen relationship if Leader listens.

---

### Choice C — “I’m asking you as Leader.”

Brindle:

> “Then I am answering you as Brindle.”

Hard refusal.

At low Trust, conversation chills.

At high Trust:

> “You know why I won't.”

Delayed callback:

If Leader later avoids using her faith instrumentally:

> “You remembered.”

Two words can be enough.

# 5. Rowan Pike — A Promise to Hearthwick

**Context:** Lanterns on the Old Road offered.

Rowan:

> “I need to know whether Hearthwick can plan around you.”

### Choice A — PROMISE: “We’ll clear the road.”

Rowan:

> “Clear means safe enough for carts.”
>
> “Not heroic.”
>
> “Not interesting.”
>
> “Safe enough for carts.”

**Promise condition:** return with road safe or explicitly renegotiate.

If kept:

> Rowan:
>
> “You said clear.”
>
> He looks toward the wagons.
>
> “You meant it.”

If the promise is explicitly broken or repudiated without renegotiation:

> Delay alone does **not** break this untimed promise.

> “Tell me what you intend to do now.”
>
> “Leave promises out of it.”

---

### Choice B — HONEST UNCERTAINTY: “We’ll investigate. I won’t promise the outcome.”

Rowan:

> “Less comforting.”
>
> “More useful.”

Later if successful:

> “You never promised me a safe road.”
>
> “You gave me one anyway.”

---

### Choice C — REFUSE: “We have other priorities.”

Rowan:

> “Then say that loudly enough that I stop waiting.”

This preserves respect better than accepting and silently ignoring him.

# 6. Oswin Marris — Before the Ledger Changes Hands

**Context:** Leader has recovered the ledger before returning to Oswin.

Oswin sees the wrapped book.

> “I assume you're here to finish the job.”

### Choice A — “Before I do, tell me who gets hurt if I return it.”

Oswin's expression stills.

> “Ah.”
>
> “So you read enough to become responsible.”

This should unlock a more candid argument than his original offer.

---

### Choice B — LIE: “We haven’t found it.”

Mira knows this is false.

Oswin:

> “Then you have developed a very specific way of holding nothing.”

If lie succeeds socially, he may not expose it immediately.

**Memory:** Leader lied to Oswin in front of party.

Mira later:

> “You lied cleanly.”
>
> Depending on Trust:
>
> “Useful.”
>
> or:
>
> “Next time tell me before I'm scenery in it.”

---

### Choice C — “I found it. I haven’t decided who gets it.”

Oswin:

> “That is not the arrangement.”

Leader:

> “It is now.”

This should change future tone even if the ledger ultimately returns to him.

---

### Choice D — “I’m hearing Nera and Holt before I decide.”

Oswin:

> “Wonderful.”
>
> “A criminal broker and a man who thinks shelving is a moral act.”

Oren:

> “That is not what Holt thinks.”

Oswin:

> “You see my problem.”

# 7. Edda Briar — The Leader Refuses a Clean Answer

**Context:** after Mosslight parley.

Edda:

> “Can I act on what you found?”

### Choice A — “Yes. Here are the routes.”

Direct exposure path.

Edda asks:

> “Anything I need to know before I send people?”

Party interjections can change final wording.

---

### Choice B — “Not yet. What you do with partial truth could be worse.”

Edda:

> “Possible.”
>
> “Also possible that waiting hurts somebody.”

She does not treat caution as cowardice.

**Memory:** Leader withheld evidence pending confidence.

Delayed callback if later harm occurs:

> “You wanted more certainty.”
>
> “Now we have more consequences.”

Not necessarily accusation.

---

### Choice C — “I’ll tell you what I saw. I won’t give you names.”

Edda:

> “Then I can plan around the problem, not arrest the people.”

Nera may later learn and react differently than to full exposure.

This is a meaningful middle position if later implementation supports it.

---

### Choice D — DEFER: “Garrick, you saw the stores. What do you think?”

Garrick may give a direct recommendation.

Mira may object to giving Warden authority through him.

The Leader remains responsible for choosing whether to adopt the advice.

# 8. Nera Vale — A Lie the Party Must Carry

**Context:** Crow is searching for Mosslight route names.

Nera:

> “If he asks whether I gave you names, what are you going to say?”

### Choice A — “The truth.”

Nera:

> “Good.”
>
> “Now tell me which truth.”
>
> “That I gave you some?”
>
> “That I kept some?”
>
> “That you didn't ask who would be hurt by the difference?”

The conversation teaches that “truth” can still be selected.

---

### Choice B — PROMISE: “I won’t give him your names.”

Mira watches carefully.

**Promise condition:** do not later disclose Nera’s names to Crow without renegotiating.

Nera:

> “Don't promise because you like the sound of being trustworthy.”

If kept:

> “You kept it.”
>
> “Annoying.”

If broken:

> “Next time sell me.”
>
> “At least then the price will be honest.”

---

### Choice C — “I won’t promise that.”

Nera:

> “Better.”

This can earn respect because the Leader avoids a false reassurance.

---

### Choice D — “Mira, what would you tell him?”

Mira:

> “Depends whether I'm answering Crow or keeping Nera alive.”

Nera:

> “See why I like her?”

This can open a three-way conversation about obligation.

# Delayed callback requirement summary

Each party member has at least one required later callback:

## Garrick

Leader's approach to accepting help / ordering risk is recalled in later camp or supply scene.

## Mira

Respecting or pressuring her boundary around the old name changes how she later volunteers information.

## Oren

Accepting or overriding his evidentiary correction changes later “sentence you can defend” callback.

## Brindle

Respecting or overriding her refusal to promise divine safety changes later trust language around faith and comfort.

These callbacks should occur **before the ending**, not only in epilogue text.

---

# Integration handoff

Later implementation should support, at minimum:

- direct Leader-authored dialogue labels rather than abstract menu verbs;
- topic memory so first-time questions become follow-ups;
- promise/lie/refusal memories for selected major conversations;
- companion interjections based on values/Trust;
- companion counterproposal/refusal states;
- delayed callbacks in at least the four party examples above;
- repeat-safe NPC dialogue;
- ability to ask questions before irreversible major decisions.

Assume the ratified active party of **Leader + three active companions**. LR-0088 remains authoring-only and does not itself implement active/reserve roster mechanics or a fixed Leader biography.
