# Lanterns on the Old Road

**Existing quest id:** `lantern_road`  
**Core identity:** protection / mystery  
**Primary NPCs:** Rowan Pike, Halden Crow  
**Primary site:** Weeping Stones

## What this quest is about

The first lesson of Lantern Road should be that a dangerous road can be made by ordinary people exploiting uncertainty.

The false-lantern crew is not the campaign's secret villain. They are a small group who discovered that frightened merchants are easier to rob when everyone already believes the road is unsafe.

The choice is not “good diplomacy versus bad violence.”

It is:

> **Do you trust a coerced local settlement enough to leave people alive and mobile, or do you remove an immediate threat by force and accept what that teaches everyone watching?**

---

# Scene 1 — Rowan's offer: The Village Pays First

Rowan should not begin with “kill bandits.”

He begins with empty space.

A delivery shelf has gaps. Tessa has fewer travellers. Someone postponed a trip to Greyfen.

### Rowan

> “Nobody has been killed yet.”
>
> “That's the sentence people keep using to tell me this isn't urgent.”
>
> He looks toward the road.
>
> “A road doesn't have to kill you to stop working.”

He explains the reports:

- lights moving where no patrol is posted;
- shouting beyond the Weeping Stones;
- merchants turning back before confirming anything;
- one driver swearing there were “ten men,” another saying “three.”

### Player responses

**Take the job immediately**

Rowan:

> “Good.”
>
> “Find out what's true before fear gets better organised than we are.”

**Ask why Wardens are not handling it**

> “Because Crow has too much road and not enough grey cloaks.”
>
> “Because Hearthwick is close enough to matter and small enough to wait.”
>
> “Pick whichever answer annoys you more.”

**Refuse for now**

Rowan does not condemn the player.

> “Then don't promise.”
>
> “I can plan around no faster than I can plan around maybe.”

**Declarative branch:** quest remains offered; later Hearthwick callback may show more wagons turning back.

# Scene 2 — Weeping Stones: Fear Has Footprints

The investigation should make the deception legible before the confrontation.

Details:

- one lantern carried in loops to simulate several moving lights;
- bootprints doubling back;
- a drag-mark where a log was used to fake a roadblock;
- cheap lamp shutters made from food tins;
- no evidence of a large organised force.

### Mira

> “Ten men don't need to walk in each other's footprints.”
>
> She crouches.
>
> “Three men pretending to be ten do.”

### Oren

He notices soot at several heights on one stone.

> “Same lamp.”
>
> Mira: “You sound disappointed.”
>
> Oren: “I dislike mysteries that become accounting.”

### Complication

A merchant's broken crate nearby contains goods but no body.

The crew is frightening people and taking abandoned cargo more often than attacking.

This makes them culpable without turning them into murderers the player is morally obliged to kill.

# Scene 3 — The crew behind the lights

The crew steps out expecting a frightened traveller.

Give one speaker a name for scene clarity: **Dace Orrin**, former wagon guard.

Do not turn Dace into a future major NPC unless LR-0048 explicitly needs it.

### Dace

> “Road's dangerous.”
>
> Mira looks at the shuttered lantern.
>
> “You seem very committed to public education.”

Dace explains enough to establish motive:

- caravan work dried up;
- tolls and inspections shifted traffic;
- frightening merchants became easier than starving;
- they tell themselves they mostly steal abandoned cargo.

Garrick:

> “You made them abandon it.”

Dace:

> “Yes.”

No euphemism after that.

# Resolution A — Break the bluff without bloodshed
**Canonical direction:** bargained / crew disperses.

Brindle or the player makes the core argument:

> The trick only works while merchants believe the crew is larger and more organised than it is.

The party can threaten to expose their method, names or route.

### Trade-off

**Benefit:** no one dies; former crew members may return to legitimate work or move elsewhere.

**Cost:** the party relies on fear, leverage and the crew keeping its word. Some may reappear.

### Dace

> “You think telling people we're three hungry idiots fixes the road?”
>
> Mira: “No.”
>
> “But it ruins your business model.”

### Immediate aftermath

The crew extinguishes the lanterns one by one.

Do not show them becoming friends.

### Turn-in to Rowan

> Rowan: “You trust them?”
>
> Player response options can include:
>
> - “No. I trust that the trick is broken.”
> - “Enough.”
> - “We know who to look for now.”

Rowan:

> “That's less comforting than I wanted.”
>
> “Probably more useful.”

### Turn-in to Crow

> “No bodies?”
>
> Crow listens.
>
> “Road open?”
>
> He listens again.
>
> “I dislike being satisfied.”

---

# Resolution B — Drive them off by force
**Canonical direction:** fought clear.

The crew reaches for steel or refuses to break.

Combat remains compact and ugly rather than triumphant.

### Pre-fight Garrick line

> “Last chance.”
>
> Dace: “You don't get to say that on our road.”
>
> Garrick: “That's the disagreement.”

### Trade-off

**Benefit:** immediate credible safety; no reliance on the crew's compliance.

**Cost:** bloodshed, possible retaliation, and the road learns that authority belongs to whoever can hold it.

### After combat

If enemies retreat/survive:

> One shuttered lantern lies crushed in the mud.
>
> It looks much smaller on the ground.

If implementation cannot track survival, keep wording neutral.

### Rowan turn-in

> “People will use the road.”
>
> Rowan looks relieved, then tired.
>
> “Somebody will also remember who you drove off it.”

### Crow turn-in

> “You cleared it.”
>
> “Do not start believing force and proof are the same thing.”

# Non-ideal branch — Back away after finding the crew

If player investigates but declines confrontation:

Dace:

> “Smart.”
>
> Mira, once out of hearing:
>
> “He thinks that means afraid.”

The quest remains active.

### Later consequence

- road fear continues;
- Hearthwick callbacks worsen;
- returning gives the crew a prepared position or different opening line.

Dace on return:

> “Changed your mind?”
>
> Player may answer:
>
> “No. Changed the odds.”

---

# Partial-success branch — Failed bargain becomes violence

If Brindle's attempt fails, the story should acknowledge why.

Not:

> Persuasion failed.

Instead:

> Dace looks at the others.
>
> “She's right.”
>
> One of them says:
>
> “And what do we eat tomorrow?”
>
> The moment breaks in the wrong direction.

Combat starts because the crew cannot see a way back, not because a die roll magically made them irrational.
