# Sister Brindle — Direct Conversation Pack

## Voice

Warm without being vague. Observant, occasionally funny, unwilling to make pain sound cleaner than it is.

She values mercy and faith.

She resists cruelty disguised as efficiency and certainty used to manipulate frightened people.

---

# Greeting states

## Guarded

> “You wanted something?”

Not cold, but careful.

## Steady

> “Sit, if you like.”

## Warming

> “You have the look.”
>
> “Question or confession?”

## Trusting

> “Tell me the part you’re avoiding.”

## Hurt

> “Yes, I know.”
>
> “Priests are allowed to be patients.”

## Exhausted

> “I can listen.”
>
> “I cannot promise wisdom tonight.”

This may close deeper emotional topics while leaving practical ones available.

# Recurring topics

## “Do you ever doubt the Lamp?”

> “Of course.”

Leader:

> “That simple?”

> “Doubt is not absence.”
>
> “It is often attention.”

If pressed:

> “What frightens me more is using faith to avoid admitting a choice is ours.”

After **What the Lamp Asks**:

- child:
  > “We chose the person with least power.”
  >
  > “That did not erase the others.”
- scout:
  > “Saving one person for the sake of many can be mercy.”
  >
  > “It can also become a very convenient sentence.”
- pilgrims:
  > “We helped people who were bad arguments.”
  >
  > “I’m glad we remembered them.”

Repeat-safe:

> “Still asking about doubt?”
>
> “Good.”
>
> “Certainty gets lonely if nobody checks on it.”

# Requests

## Treatment request

Leader:

> “Let someone treat that.”

### Trusting

> “Yes.”
>
> “And thank you for asking before ordering.”

### Guarded

> “I know what I can carry.”

Leader may:

- ask what she needs;
- offer supplies;
- command in emergency;
- accept refusal if safe.

If she has been caring for everyone else while hurt:

> “All right.”
>
> “Before I become a sermon about hypocrisy.”

## Rest request

Leader:

> “You’re resting.”

Exhausted:

> “Gladly.”

If she is staying awake for someone distressed:

> “I will rest after they settle.”

Counter-offer:

> “Sit with them for ten minutes.”
>
> “Then I’ll sleep.”

This lets the Leader share emotional labour.

## Risky plan request

Leader:

> “Tell the frightened travellers the Lamp will protect the crossing.”

Hard value refusal:

> “No.”
>
> “I can tell them I’ll walk it with them.”
>
> “I will not make faith into your guarantee.”

High Trust does not change the answer.

# Advice after failure

If Leader returns to people harmed:

> “Let them be angry.”
>
> “Do not make them comfort us for regretting it.”

If Leader wants reassurance:

> “I can tell you why the choice made sense.”
>
> “I cannot tell you nobody was hurt.”
