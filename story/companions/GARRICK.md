# Garrick Vale — Direct Conversation Pack

## Voice

Short, practical, dry. He rarely performs emotion verbally.

He respects shared burden, clear responsibility and people who face the consequence of their own orders.

He resists being treated as expendable simply because he is willing to stand in front.

---

# Greeting states

## Guarded

> “Need something?”

If recently commanded into unnecessary danger:

> “Say what you need.”
>
> “Not what you think I’ll agree to.”

## Steady

> “What is it?”

If camp is safe:

> “We have a little time.”

## Warming

> “You look like you’re about to make this my problem.”
>
> “Go on.”

## Trusting

> “Ask.”
>
> “I’ll tell you if I think it’s a bad idea.”

## Hurt

> “I’m fine.”
>
> The pause lasts too long.

Leader options:

- “That wasn’t the question.”
- “Use a bandage.”
- “All right. Tell me if it changes.”

## Exhausted

> “If this is about watch order, I already hate your answer.”

# Recurring topics

## “Why do you always stand in front?”

First time:

> “Because somebody has to.”
>
> “And because I’m good at it.”

Probe:

> “That’s the useful answer.”

Warming/Trusting deeper line:

> “The worse answer is that I know who I am when I’m useful.”

After **The Wall That Walks**:

- shared-duty outcome:
  > “I’m learning to ask who else can carry it.”
- self-sacrifice reinforced:
  > “Still standing in front.”
  >
  > “Just trying not to mistake that for being the whole wall.”
- stepped-back outcome:
  > “Turns out the road keeps moving when I’m not first through every door.”

Repeat-safe:

> “Still asking why I stand in front?”
>
> “Ask me again after I stop.”

# Requests

## Treatment request

Leader:

> “Use a bandage.”

### High Trust / serious injury

> “All right.”
>
> “No ceremony.”

### Low Trust / moderate injury

> “Save it.”
>
> “I can still stand.”

Leader follow-ups:

- **Explain**: “I need you standing tomorrow too.”
  - may accept.
- **Share burden**: “I’m using one too.”
  - more likely to accept.
- **Command**: “That’s an order.”
  - accepts if urgent, possible Trust cost.
- **Accept refusal**.
  - remembered if he worsens.

## Rest request

Leader:

> “You’re off first watch.”

### Warming/Trusting

> “Only if you take second.”

### Guarded

> “Put Mira on first and I’ll consider it.”

Counter-offer preserves his protective instinct.

## Risky plan request

Leader:

> “Hold the doorway while we cross.”

If risk is proportionate:

> “Fine.”
>
> “Move when I say move.”

If plan is needless or repeatedly uses him as expendable:

> “No.”
>
> “Find a plan that doesn’t begin with ‘Garrick absorbs it.’”

# Advice after failure

If a quest outcome goes badly and Leader returns to affected people:

> “Good.”
>
> “Now stay for the part where they’re angry.”

If Leader wants to avoid the return:

> “No.”
>
> “We go back.”

This is a value refusal, not random obstruction.
